============================================================
1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

This document defines the proposed foundational safety, control,
ownership, emergency governance, and succession architecture
for the SANOUR platform.

SANOUR is intended to be a modular, provider-independent AI
platform. Its future capabilities may include AI models, Agents,
tools, external providers, APIs, memory, automation, and
multilingual services.

The purpose of this architecture is to ensure that:

- AI systems cannot take control of their own governance.
- Agents cannot expand their own authority without authorization.
- Independent safety mechanisms remain outside AI control.
- Human users and legitimate administrators can continue
  performing normal work wherever safe.
- Sensitive operations remain protected during failures.
- Ownership and emergency succession are governed by explicit,
  traceable rules.
- No single AI, Agent, administrator, or unauthorized person can
  remove all independent safety protections.

This document defines architectural principles and approved
owner decisions. It does not define or authorize implementation.


============================================================
2. FOUNDATIONAL GOVERNANCE PRINCIPLE
============================================================

No AI system, Agent, autonomous process, or external provider
may independently change, remove, weaken, disable, or bypass
SANOUR's independent control and safety mechanisms.

Final governance authority must remain outside AI control.

Accordingly:

1. AI cannot modify its own foundational security policies.
2. AI cannot increase its own privileges without authorization.
3. AI cannot create Agents with greater privileges without
   authorization.
4. AI cannot disable or bypass independent safety controls.
5. Sensitive actions require valid authorization before execution.
6. An independent mechanism must be capable of stopping or
   limiting AI activities.
7. Restarting sensitive AI activities after a dangerous incident
   requires human review and appropriate authorization.

No absolute security guarantee is claimed. Compromise of the
operating system, hardware, credentials, cryptographic keys,
or independent control infrastructure remains a possible risk
that must be addressed through technical design.


============================================================
3. SEPARATION OF AUTHORITY DOMAINS
============================================================

The proposed SANOUR architecture separates three domains.

3.1 AI DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Reasoning and analysis.
- Processing authorized tasks.
- Using permitted tools.
- Producing recommendations and outputs.
- Operating within assigned permissions and resource limits.

The AI domain cannot govern or modify the independent safety
domain.

3.2 INDEPENDENT CONTROL AND SAFETY DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Authorization enforcement.
- Access control.
- Resource and execution limits.
- Restrictions on sensitive operations.
- Emergency stop and containment.
- Independent control of AI and Agent access to tools,
  providers, networks, and sensitive operations.

This domain must not depend on AI decisions for its own
security enforcement.

3.3 HUMAN GOVERNANCE DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Approving foundational policies.
- Defining authorized roles.
- Approving exceptional permissions.
- Reviewing serious incidents.
- Approving changes to foundational safety rules.
- Governing ownership and succession.

AI systems and Agents cannot replace this authority.


============================================================
4. INDEPENDENT EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
============================================================

SANOUR shall be designed with an independent Emergency Safety
Layer.

This layer must be:

- Independent of AI reasoning and Agent decisions.
- Bounded in authority.
- Designed to control high-risk operations.
- Capable of limiting or stopping AI access to sensitive tools,
  providers, networks, and operations.
- Protected against unauthorized modification.
- Subject to human governance.

It must not become an unrestricted authority that can
unnecessarily block ordinary user activity.

4.1 PROPORTIONAL CONTROL

Restrictions must be limited to the smallest affected scope.

Preferred restriction order:

1. Restrict the affected Agent.
2. Restrict the affected Provider or execution path.
3. Preserve healthy independent user services.
4. Stop a wider system only when systemic risk requires it.

Every restriction should record:

- Cause.
- Affected scope.
- Remaining available functions.
- Responsible authority.
- Start time.
- Review time.
- Conditions required to remove the restriction.

4.2 PROTECTION OF NORMAL USERS

Emergency controls must not unnecessarily prevent ordinary
users or legitimate administrators from using healthy services.

The safety layer controls AI and high-risk operations, not
unrelated healthy user functions.

Security restrictions must be proportional and scope-limited.


============================================================
5. FAILURE OF THE EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
============================================================

Approved architectural principle:

FAIL SAFE, NOT FAIL OPEN.

If the Emergency Safety Layer becomes unavailable:

1. Sensitive operations must stop or remain restricted until
   valid control is restored.
2. Healthy and independent low-risk services may continue
   where their safety can be established.
3. Agents cannot acquire new privileges or permissions.
4. Providers and external tools cannot initiate new sensitive
   operations without valid authorization.
5. Evidence and records relating to the failure must be preserved.
6. Resumption of sensitive operations requires review of the
   control layer's health and approval by an authorized human.

The safety architecture should avoid making the Emergency Safety
Layer a single point of failure.

However, the technical method for achieving resilience,
redundancy, independent monitoring, and safe degradation
requires a separate technical design.

No automatic recovery or permission expansion is authorized.


============================================================
6. HUMAN AND ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
============================================================

Administrative access must not equal unlimited authority.

The following principles apply:

1. An ordinary administrator cannot disable the Emergency
   Safety Layer.
2. Changes to foundational safety policies require approval
   from the SANOUR architecture owner or an explicitly
   designated governance authority.
3. Highly sensitive operations should require approval from
   two independent people where feasible.
4. Important administrative decisions must be recorded.
5. No administrator or Agent may independently remove all
   safety protections.
6. Administrative privileges must be limited to their defined
   responsibilities.

The detailed role model, authentication mechanisms, approval
workflow, and permission matrix remain subject to technical
design.


============================================================
7. PROTECTION OF THE OWNER'S ACCOUNT
============================================================

The owner's account is a critical governance identity.

The following protections are approved as architectural
requirements:

1. Strong multi-factor authentication.
2. Re-authentication for critical changes.
3. Independent recovery keys stored securely and offline.
4. Security delays for highly sensitive changes, with an
   opportunity to cancel them.
5. Traceable records of requests, approvals, and outcomes.
6. Suspicious critical changes must be suspended pending
   human review.
7. These controls must not unnecessarily complicate ordinary
   user access.

Possession of the owner's ordinary account credentials alone
must not automatically provide unrestricted authority to
change every foundational safety mechanism.

Technical details of authentication, key custody, delay
periods, and suspicious-activity detection remain unresolved.


============================================================
8. EMERGENCY OWNER RECOVERY
============================================================

If the owner loses access to a password, authentication device,
or primary key, SANOUR must provide an emergency recovery path.

Approved principles:

1. Recovery must be independent of AI and Agent decisions.
2. Recovery keys must be securely maintained offline.
3. Identity recovery requires multiple independent forms of
   evidence or authentication factors.
4. Ordinary administrators and Agents cannot appoint a new owner.
5. All recovery steps must be recorded and auditable.
6. Critical authority must not be transferred before identity
   verification is completed.

The detailed recovery procedure, evidence standards, custody
arrangements, and applicable legal requirements require
separate design.


============================================================
9. EMERGENCY SUCCESSION AND PERMANENT OWNERSHIP
============================================================

Emergency succession and permanent ownership transfer are
separate processes.

9.1 EMERGENCY SUCCESSION

The owner may designate an emergency successor or trustee
in advance.

The successor's authority is limited to the defined emergency
responsibilities required to preserve SANOUR's security and
continuity.

The emergency successor:

- Does not automatically become permanent owner.
- Cannot transfer permanent ownership.
- Cannot weaken foundational safety policies.
- Cannot bypass Zero Automatic Retry.
- Cannot appoint a new permanent owner.
- Cannot use emergency authority to expand personal privileges.

9.2 ACTIVATION OF EMERGENCY SUCCESSION

The owner's absence alone does not activate the successor.

Activation requires:

1. Verification of the owner's unavailability under
   predefined conditions.
2. Independent verification of the successor's identity.
3. Approval by an independent authorized human authority.

No Agent may activate, cancel, or modify the successor's mandate.

9.3 END OF EMERGENCY SUCCESSION

Ending the emergency mandate also requires independent
human approval.

The owner's return does not automatically erase records
or silently transfer authority.

The beginning, scope, decisions, and end of the emergency
mandate must remain traceable.


============================================================
10. PERMANENT SUCCESSION
============================================================

Permanent ownership transfer is distinct from emergency
succession.

Approved principles:

1. An emergency successor does not automatically become
   permanent owner.
2. Permanent transfer requires a valid succession document
   established and approved in advance by the owner.
3. The validity of the document must be assessed using
   independent evidence.
4. If no valid document exists, transfer must stop.
5. If a material dispute exists, transfer must stop pending
   proper resolution.
6. AI systems, Agents, and ordinary administrators cannot
   appoint a new permanent owner.
7. Applicable laws and the legal rights of relevant persons
   must be respected.

A permanent transfer cannot be inferred from technical
possession of an account, device, key, or server.


============================================================
11. INDEPENDENT PERMANENT SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
============================================================

A separate authority shall be designated to review and
approve permanent ownership succession.

This authority must be independent of:

- AI systems and Agents.
- Ordinary system administration.
- Any individual applicant seeking ownership.

Its responsibilities include:

1. Reviewing the succession document.
2. Reviewing relevant legal and identity evidence.
3. Identifying conflicts or disputed claims.
4. Suspending transfer when evidence is insufficient.
5. Recording its review and final decision.
6. Maintaining an auditable record of the process.

Approval of succession does not automatically grant the
authority permission to modify foundational safety policies.

The legal status, jurisdiction, and formal constitution of
this authority require separate legal and governance design.


============================================================
12. MEMBERSHIP OF THE SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
============================================================

Approved structure:

- Three principal members.
- Two designated alternate members.

12.1 APPOINTMENT

The owner appoints the initial members.

Alternates are designated in advance.

AI systems and Agents have no role in selecting, appointing,
or dismissing members.

12.2 VOTING

A permanent ownership transfer requires at least two
affirmative votes from three valid and eligible members.

No single member can approve a transfer independently.

A member with a conflict of interest cannot vote on the
affected case.

If the number of valid and independent members is insufficient,
the transfer must stop.

12.3 INDEPENDENCE

Multiple accounts alone do not establish independent approval.

Members must have:

- Independently verifiable identities.
- Independent authentication methods.
- Separate individual credentials.
- No shared approval credentials.
- A duty to disclose relevant conflicts of interest.

No Agent may cast a vote or approve a decision on behalf
of a member.

Evidence of collusion or material conflict requires suspension
of the sensitive decision and human review.


============================================================
13. MEMBER QUALIFICATION
============================================================

Members must:

1. Have a verifiable real identity.
2. Declare independence and relevant conflicts of interest.
3. Understand their legal and security responsibilities.
4. Maintain independent accounts and authentication methods.
5. Never delegate their vote to an Agent or another person.
6. Remain qualified throughout their appointment.

Family, friendship, or professional relationships alone
do not automatically establish or disqualify eligibility.

Actual independence and case-specific conflicts must be
assessed.

Loss of qualification requires replacement through the
recorded governance process.


============================================================
14. RESIGNATION, REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT
============================================================

Approved principles:

1. A member may formally resign.
2. Death, incapacity, or loss of qualification may trigger
   the replacement review process.
3. Removal requires documented grounds and an independent
   procedure.
4. No member may unilaterally remove another member.
5. A member under review cannot participate in the sensitive
   decision affected by that review.
6. Changes in membership must be recorded.
7. Historical decisions and records must be preserved.
8. If enough qualified members are unavailable, permanent
   transfer remains suspended.

14.1 ALTERNATE MEMBERS

An alternate initially completes only the remaining term
of the replaced principal member.

Entry into the authority requires formal recording and
a renewed qualification review.

Any subsequent independent appointment begins a new
five-year term only after valid selection.

Replacement must not create automatic or unlimited extensions
of authority.


============================================================
15. TERMS OF APPOINTMENT
============================================================

Approved appointment structure:

- Principal member term: five years.
- One renewal may be permitted after independent evaluation.
- Qualification and conflict-of-interest review: annually.
- Alternate members must maintain their qualifications.

Expiry of a term does not itself create new authority.

If the authority does not have enough valid members, it
cannot approve permanent ownership transfer until a valid
composition is restored.

The process for evaluating renewal and appointing replacements
requires further governance documentation.


============================================================
16. DISAGREEMENT AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION
============================================================

If members disagree on a sensitive decision:

1. No member may independently execute the decision.
2. The request remains suspended until the disagreement
   is properly resolved.
3. The matter is referred to a higher authorized governance
   authority.
4. Evidence, objections, and reasons from each side are
   preserved.
5. Silence or passage of time is not approval.
6. If the disagreement remains unresolved, the sensitive
   operation does not proceed.
7. Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

No AI system or Agent may resolve a governance disagreement
by independently selecting a winning position.


============================================================
17. ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY AND SAFETY GOVERNANCE
============================================================

This architecture must remain consistent with SANOUR's
approved Zero Automatic Retry principle.

The initial authorized logical effect is the first attempt,
admitted under existing admission and governance rules.

Any subsequent external write or delivery for the same
logical effect is a subsequent attempt.

System-initiated subsequent attempts are prohibited.

A subsequent attempt requires its own specific, recorded,
per-instance authorization under separate governance.

This principle includes:

- Financial and external effects.
- Provider requests.
- Audit Sink delivery.
- Lease expiry and reclaim paths.
- Startup replay.
- Redelivery and resubmission.

UNKNOWN never automatically becomes failure or permission
to resend.

No emergency, succession, administrative, or ownership
mechanism may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

The detailed authorization mechanism remains subject to
separate governance and technical design.


============================================================
18. AUDITABILITY AND EVIDENCE
============================================================

Important governance and safety events must be traceable.

Records should preserve, where applicable:

- Actor identity.
- Action requested.
- Scope of authority.
- Approval or refusal.
- Reason.
- Evidence reference.
- Time of request and decision.
- Relevant affected operation.
- Result and subsequent review.

Historical records must not be silently overwritten or deleted.

Corrections must be recorded as separate traceable events
linked to the original record.

Sensitive credentials, passwords, API keys, and raw
authentication secrets must never be stored in audit records.

Access to confidential audit information must itself be
auditable.

Technical storage, cryptographic integrity, retention,
access control, and verification mechanisms require
separate design.


============================================================
19. INCIDENT RESPONSE AND SAFE HALT
============================================================

When a serious safety, security, accounting, or audit
incident occurs, SANOUR must be capable of restricting
the affected operation or module.

The preferred approach is to stop only the affected scope,
not the entire system, when safe isolation is possible.

Approved priority order:

1. Prevent unintended or duplicate financial effects.
2. Preserve audit evidence and history.
3. Contain security risk.
4. Preserve healthy independent services.
5. Recover only after review, evidence, and authorization.

A partially completed transaction must not be automatically
completed or repeated.

UNKNOWN must not be automatically changed.

19.1 SAFE RESUME

Resuming an affected sensitive operation requires:

- The cause to be identified.
- Credible evidence that the risk has been resolved.
- The affected operation's status to be assessed.
- The risk of unintended repetition or external effect to
  be addressed.
- An authorized person to record the reason and approval.

Resuming a service does not authorize repeating an old
external operation.

UNKNOWN remains subject to its own evidence and decision rules.

19.2 RESUME AUTHORITY

The proposed responsibility mapping is:

- Financial/accounting risk: authorized finance or accounting lead.
- Security risk: security lead.
- Audit/evidence integrity risk: audit lead.
- Cross-domain or conflicting authority: architecture owner
  or governance authority designated by the owner.

None of these authorities may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.
