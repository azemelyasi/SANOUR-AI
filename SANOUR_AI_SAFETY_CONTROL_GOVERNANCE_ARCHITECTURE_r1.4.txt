============================================================
0. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

The purpose of this document is to establish a unified architectural
and governance framework for the safety, control, auditability,
ownership, and responsible operation of SANOUR.

SANOUR is intended to be a modular, provider-agnostic AI platform.
Its architecture must not depend on the assumption that an AI model,
Agent, provider, or autonomous process will always behave correctly.

The system must preserve independent human governance and independent
safety controls.

This document covers:

- AI authority limitations.
- Independent safety and control mechanisms.
- Human ownership and governance.
- Owner account protection and recovery.
- Emergency succession and permanent ownership transfer.
- Zero Automatic Retry.
- External effects and UNKNOWN outcomes.
- Audit Event identity and Audit Sink delivery.
- Read-only operation status and history.
- Four-database target architecture.
- Backup and migration principles.
- Usage reservations.
- Audit integrity, confidentiality, continuity, and retention.
- Safe Halt and Safe Resume.
- Implementation authorization boundaries.

This document does not claim that the described architecture has
already been implemented or independently verified.

============================================================
1. FOUNDATIONAL SAFETY PRINCIPLE
============================================================

No AI system, Agent, model, provider, or autonomous process may have
authority to change, remove, weaken, or bypass SANOUR's independent
control and safety mechanisms.

Final governance authority remains outside AI control.

An AI system must not:

1. Change its own foundational security policies.
2. Increase its own privileges without valid authorization.
3. Create or authorize Agents with greater privileges without valid
   authorization.
4. Disable or bypass independent safety mechanisms.
5. Appoint or replace the system owner.
6. Change ownership or succession rules.
7. Approve its own sensitive operations.
8. Override Zero Automatic Retry.
9. Modify or erase its own audit history.
10. Decide that a missing authorization may be ignored.

AI may assist with analysis, recommendations, explanations, and
preparation of decisions. Assistance does not constitute authority.

Sensitive actions require valid authorization before execution.

An independent mechanism must retain the ability to restrict or stop
AI access to sensitive operations.

============================================================
2. AUTHORITY DOMAINS
============================================================

The following three-domain separation is a proposed architectural
model and remains subject to formal architectural approval:

A. AI DOMAIN

Responsible for:
- User-facing AI functionality.
- Model and provider interaction.
- Agent execution within assigned permissions.
- Analysis and recommendations.
- Authorized task execution.

The AI Domain cannot govern or modify the independent safety layer.

B. INDEPENDENT CONTROL AND SAFETY DOMAIN

Responsible for:
- Enforcing permission boundaries.
- Restricting sensitive operations.
- Applying safety policies.
- Preventing unauthorized privilege escalation.
- Supporting independent stop and restriction mechanisms.
- Preserving safety evidence.

The exact technical implementation remains unresolved.

C. HUMAN GOVERNANCE DOMAIN

Responsible for:
- Ownership and governance decisions.
- Approval of foundational safety policy changes.
- Appointment of authorized human roles.
- Emergency recovery and succession decisions.
- Resolution of governance disputes.
- Authorization of sensitive changes.

AI systems and Agents cannot replace this domain.

============================================================
3. EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE:

SANOUR must have safety and control mechanisms that are independent
of AI systems and Agents.

The ordinary administrator or an Agent must not be able to disable
the entire independent safety mechanism.

The system must preserve an independent ability to restrict or stop
AI access to tools, network access, and sensitive operations.

PROPOSED TECHNICAL CHARACTERISTICS:

The Emergency Safety Layer should be:

- Independent of AI decision-making.
- Small and understandable where practical.
- Bounded in scope and authority.
- Protected against unauthorized modification.
- Capable of restricting sensitive access.
- Designed to avoid unnecessary dependence on a single component.
- Designed so that failure does not grant additional AI authority.

The exact design, implementation, redundancy, and failure-detection
mechanisms remain open technical decisions.

The Emergency Safety Layer must not become a second autonomous AI
authority.

============================================================
4. PROPORTIONAL SAFETY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE:

Safety controls should be proportional to the risk.

SANOUR should restrict the smallest affected scope necessary.

Preferred restriction order:

1. Affected Agent.
2. Affected provider or execution path.
3. Affected sensitive operation.
4. Affected module.
5. Broader system restriction only when risk is systemic.

Healthy and independent services should continue where safe.

A restriction record should identify:

- Cause.
- Affected scope.
- Start time.
- Remaining permitted functions.
- Responsible authority.
- Evidence.
- Review conditions.
- Conditions for lifting the restriction.

A restriction must not silently change the status of an unrelated
operation.

============================================================
5. FAIL SAFE, NOT FAIL OPEN
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE:

If an essential safety mechanism fails, SANOUR must not respond by
granting additional permissions or allowing sensitive operations
without valid control.

When the safety layer is unavailable or cannot establish a valid
control decision:

- Sensitive operations must stop or remain restricted.
- Agents must not gain new privileges.
- Providers and tools must not receive new sensitive operations
  without valid authorization.
- Evidence must be preserved.
- Healthy independent low-risk services may continue where safe.
- Sensitive operations may resume only after review and valid
  human authorization.

The architecture should avoid creating a single point of failure
where practical.

Avoiding a single point of failure must not weaken the independent
control requirement.

============================================================
6. ADMINISTRATOR AND PRIVILEGE CONTROL
============================================================

An ordinary administrator must not be able to disable the Emergency
Safety Layer.

An ordinary administrator must not be able to remove all independent
safety controls.

Foundational safety policy changes require approval from the owner
or a properly designated governance authority.

Highly sensitive operations should require two independent human
approvals where feasible.

Important administrative decisions must be recorded.

An administrator's operational role does not automatically grant
authority over ownership, succession, or foundational safety policy.

Detailed role definitions, authentication requirements, and
permission matrices remain subject to technical and governance design.

============================================================
7. OWNER ACCOUNT PROTECTION
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED REQUIREMENTS:

The owner account must receive stronger protection than ordinary
user accounts.

Required principles:

1. Strong multi-factor authentication.
2. Re-authentication for critical changes.
3. Independent offline recovery keys.
4. A security delay for highly sensitive changes, with an opportunity
   to cancel.
5. Logging of requests, approvals, and outcomes.
6. Suspension of suspicious critical changes pending human review.
7. No unnecessary burden on ordinary user access.

The owner account must not be recoverable solely through an AI Agent,
ordinary administrator, or a process controlled by the account
whose authority is being recovered.

Exact authentication technologies and recovery procedures remain
technical design matters.

============================================================
8. EMERGENCY OWNER RECOVERY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLES:

Emergency owner recovery must be independent of AI and Agents.

It must use:

- Secure offline recovery keys.
- Multiple independent forms of evidence or factors.
- Human verification.
- Traceable recovery records.

An ordinary administrator or Agent cannot appoint a new owner.

No critical ownership transfer may proceed before verification
requirements are satisfied.

All recovery actions must be recorded.

Emergency recovery does not automatically authorize permanent
ownership transfer.

============================================================
9. EMERGENCY SUCCESSION
============================================================

The owner may designate an emergency successor or trustee in advance.

The emergency successor receives limited emergency authority only.

Emergency succession must not automatically:

- Transfer permanent ownership.
- Appoint a permanent owner.
- Weaken foundational safety policies.
- Override Zero Automatic Retry.
- Expand the successor's authority beyond the defined emergency scope.

Activation requires:

1. Verification that the owner is unavailable under predefined
   conditions.
2. Independent verification of the successor's identity.
3. Independent human approval.
4. A traceable activation record.

Termination also requires independent human approval.

The owner's return does not automatically erase or transfer decisions
made during the emergency period.

AI cannot activate, cancel, modify, or extend emergency succession.

============================================================
10. PERMANENT OWNERSHIP TRANSFER
============================================================

Permanent ownership transfer is separate from emergency succession.

An emergency successor does not automatically become the permanent
owner.

Permanent transfer requires:

- A valid succession document prepared or approved in advance.
- Independent evidence.
- Verification of the applicable succession conditions.
- Review by the independent succession authority.
- Required qualified human approvals.

If the required document is absent, invalid, or disputed, transfer
must stop.

AI, Agents, and ordinary administrators cannot appoint a new owner.

Applicable laws and jurisdictional requirements must be respected.

============================================================
11. INDEPENDENT SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
============================================================

An independent succession authority must operate independently
of AI, Agents, ordinary administrators, and the person seeking
ownership transfer.

Its responsibilities include:

- Reviewing succession documents.
- Reviewing legal and identity evidence.
- Evaluating conflicts and independence.
- Stopping a transfer when evidence is insufficient or disputed.
- Recording the decision and supporting evidence.

Succession authority does not automatically grant authority to
change foundational safety policies.

The legal constitution, jurisdiction, and formal establishment
of this authority remain unresolved.

============================================================
12. SUCCESSION AUTHORITY MEMBERSHIP
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED STRUCTURE:

- Three principal members.
- Two alternates.
- The owner appoints the initial members.
- Alternates are designated in advance.
- AI has no role in selecting or dismissing members.

A permanent ownership transfer requires at least two affirmative
votes from three valid and eligible principal members.

No single member may approve the transfer alone.

A member with a relevant conflict of interest cannot vote on that
matter.

If fewer than the required number of valid eligible members are
available, the transfer must stop.

Member identities and authentication must be independent.

Multiple accounts alone do not establish independent identity.

Shared credentials are prohibited.

An Agent cannot vote or act as a member.

Credible evidence of collusion requires suspension of the affected
decision and independent human review.

============================================================
13. MEMBER QUALIFICATION
============================================================

A qualified member must:

- Have a verifiable real identity.
- Declare relevant conflicts of interest.
- Demonstrate independence.
- Understand the legal and security responsibilities.
- Use independent accounts and authentication.
- Maintain qualification over time.
- Not delegate their vote to an Agent or another person.

Family, friendship, or professional relationships alone neither
automatically qualify nor disqualify a person.

Actual independence and conflicts must be evaluated.

============================================================
14. RESIGNATION, REMOVAL, AND REPLACEMENT
============================================================

Resignation must be formal and recorded.

Death, incapacity, or loss of qualification triggers a review.

Removal requires documented grounds and an independent process.

No member may unilaterally remove another member.

A member under review must not participate in the sensitive decision
that concerns that member.

Membership changes must be recorded while preserving previous records.

If valid membership becomes insufficient, permanent transfer stops.

An alternate first completes the remaining term of the replaced
member.

A new independent five-year term may begin only after a valid
selection process.

============================================================
15. MEMBER TERMS AND REVIEW
============================================================

Principal member term: five years.

One renewal is permitted after independent evaluation.

Qualification and conflict-of-interest reviews must occur annually.

Alternates must maintain their qualifications.

Expiry of a term does not create new authority.

If the required valid membership is not available, permanent
ownership transfer must stop.

============================================================
16. DISAGREEMENT AND DEADLOCK
============================================================

No individual member may independently execute a disputed
ownership transfer.

A disputed request must be suspended pending resolution.

The matter must be escalated to the higher governance authority
where one is validly designated.

Objections, evidence, and reasons must be preserved.

Silence, delay, or passage of time does not constitute approval.

If disagreement remains unresolved, the sensitive operation
does not proceed.

Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

AI cannot choose the winning side or resolve a governance dispute
by itself.

============================================================
17. ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY — ZR-DEF
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DEFINITION: P1-A

An initial authorized logical effect or request is the first attempt.
It is admitted under the existing admission and governance rules.

A subsequent attempt is any further external write or delivery
for the same logical effect, identified by:

effect_type + logical_key

System-initiated subsequent attempts are prohibited.

A timer- or scheduler-initiated subsequent attempt is automatic,
even if an operator previously configured the timer or scheduler.

A human-triggered subsequent attempt is not automatically authorized.

Manual resubmission is not authorized without separate governance.

UNKNOWN must never automatically become FAILED or become permission
to resend.

A late result from the original worker is not a new attempt.

Idempotency does not authorize another attempt.

Lease expiry, startup replay, and redelivery are subject to this
definition whenever they initiate a new subsequent external effect.

Each subsequent attempt requires its own specific, recorded,
per-instance authorization under separate governance.

P1-A does not authorize implementation of retry or redelivery
mechanisms.

P1-A does not authorize any actual subsequent attempt.

============================================================
18. ATTEMPT-START BOUNDARY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P1-C1

An attempt is considered started when its attempt marker is durably
written.

This is an accounting and governance boundary.

The marker does not prove that:

- A physical network send occurred.
- A provider received the request.
- An external system accepted the request.
- The intended external effect completed.

No operational state machine is approved by this definition.

============================================================
19. AUDIT EVENT IDENTITY AND EFFECT IDENTITY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P1-C2 and P3-A

Each internal Audit Event has its own independent logical identity.

The identities must remain distinct:

- audit_event_id: identity of the logical internal Audit Event.
- outbox_key: delivery identity.
- payload_digest: payload integrity identifier.
- effect identity: identity of the external logical effect.

These identifiers are not interchangeable.

Redelivery does not create a new logical Audit Event.

Audit Event identity must not be used to bypass Zero Automatic Retry
or authorize another external effect.

============================================================
20. AUDIT SINK DELIVERY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISIONS: P1-B and P3-B

Zero Automatic Retry applies to Audit Sink delivery.

Automatic Audit Sink redelivery is not authorized.

Lease expiry does not authorize automatic redelivery.

Startup replay does not authorize automatic redelivery.

UNKNOWN delivery state remains UNKNOWN until assessed through
credible evidence and authorized human determination.

Delivery state and logical Audit Event status are separate concepts.

Evidence must be used to assess a previous delivery.

Any subsequent Audit Sink delivery requires its own independent
authorization.

These decisions do not authorize modification, disabling, or
operation of existing S3-2 mechanisms.

============================================================
21. MANUAL AUDIT SINK REDELIVERY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P3-C

Each subsequent Audit Sink delivery requires a specific,
recorded, per-event and per-attempt human authorization.

The record must include:

- Approver identity.
- Authorization time.
- Reason.
- Supporting evidence.
- Event identity.
- Attempt identity.

An authorization is not reusable for a later attempt.

Audit Sink redelivery permission does not authorize repetition
of the original external effect.

This decision does not itself authorize implementation or execution
of a redelivery mechanism.

============================================================
22. LEASE EXPIRY AND RECLAIM
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P2-C1

Lease expiry alone never authorizes a Reclaim operation that initiates
a subsequent external effect.

Lease expiry is not evidence that the previous external effect failed.

UNKNOWN remains UNKNOWN.

Any later attempt is subject to ZR-DEF and its separate
per-instance authorization requirement.

============================================================
23. STARTUP REPLAY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P2-C2

Automatic replay or re-send on restart is not authorized.

UNKNOWN must be preserved.

Status checking does not authorize resending.

A restart must not silently initiate a new external effect.

============================================================
24. CLAIM AND SEND SEPARATION
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE: S3-3

Claim alone is not authorization to Send.

Send requires independent valid authorization for the same event
and the same attempt.

Claim must not bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

UNRESOLVED:

Whether Claim without Send is allowed remains undecided.

No implementation or operational behavior may be inferred from
this unresolved question.

============================================================
25. EVIDENCE AND UNKNOWN DETERMINATION
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISIONS: P2-D1 and P2-D2

Only credible and traceable evidence linked to the exact operation
may support a final determination.

Evidence evaluation must consider:

1. Identity of the operation.
2. Source of evidence.
3. Authenticity.
4. Integrity.
5. Time and provenance.

The following alone are not sufficient proof of failure:

- Timeout.
- Lease expiry.
- Missing provider response.
- Missing receipt.
- Missing record.
- Temporary database unavailability.

A provider response indicating ACCEPTED does not necessarily prove
that the intended external effect COMPLETED.

The following states must remain distinguishable:

- ACCEPTED
- COMPLETED
- FAILED
- UNKNOWN

Final UNKNOWN determination belongs to an authorized human.

The decision record must include:

- Approver.
- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Decision time.

A worker or scheduler cannot determine final UNKNOWN status through
guesswork.

A human decision alone does not authorize resubmission.

============================================================
26. AUDIT HISTORY AND CORRECTIONS
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DECISION: P2-D3

Audit history must not be silently overwritten or deleted.

Corrections and new determinations must be recorded as separate,
traceable events linked to the relevant original record.

A correction must identify:

- Corrector.
- Time.
- Reason.
- Supporting evidence.
- Original record reference.

The original record must remain preserved.

============================================================
27. R-2A — READ-ONLY OPERATION STATUS
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED SCOPE:

R-2A is a read-only status and history capability for authorized
internal operators.

It must not perform:

- Writes.
- State transitions.
- Recovery.
- Repair.
- Lease reclaim.
- Redelivery.
- Resubmission.
- Provider refresh.
- Automatic correction.

The status view is a composite observation across the four target
databases.

Each displayed field must identify:

- Its authoritative source.
- Its last observation time.
- Its freshness or staleness.
- Whether the value is missing.
- Whether the source is unavailable.
- Whether sources disagree.

The view must show incomplete, stale, inconsistent, and unavailable
information honestly.

It must never infer success or failure from missing information.

If a database is inaccessible, it must be shown as unavailable.

The system must not infer that the other three databases provide
a complete or definitive answer.

If sources disagree:

- Show each source's observation separately.
- Mark the composite status INCONSISTENT.
- Do not automatically select a winning source.
- Do not treat the newest timestamp alone as proof of authority.
- Require review and evidence for adjudication.

============================================================
28. R-2A — OPERATION HISTORY
============================================================

The read-only history should expose traceable events where available:

- Initial request registration time.
- Attempt-start time.
- Provider response, if present.
- Audit Event registration time.
- Last observation time.
- Human UNKNOWN decision, including reason and evidence.

Each event must identify its source and time.

Missing events must be shown as missing, not reconstructed by guesswork.

History must not overwrite earlier observations or determinations.

============================================================
29. R-2A — ACCESS ROLES
============================================================

The following are approved role-scope principles:

VIEWER:
- May view information permitted to the Viewer role.
- Does not receive change, retry, repair, or recovery authority.

AUDITOR:
- May view permitted information and Audit History.
- Does not receive change, retry, repair, or recovery authority.

ADMINISTRATOR:
- May view overall status and management reports within the
  Administrator's authorized scope.
- Does not receive change, retry, repair, or recovery authority
  merely by holding the Administrator role.

Role definitions and detailed permission matrices remain technical
and governance design matters.

Observation permissions do not imply operational authority.

============================================================
30. ACCESS AUDITING AND CONFIDENTIALITY
============================================================

Access to sensitive information must itself be auditable.

An access record should include:

- User identity.
- Access time.
- Data category.
- Operation identity, where applicable.
- Allow or deny result.

Access logs must avoid unnecessary sensitive information.

Passwords, API keys, authentication secrets, and equivalent
credentials must never be stored as raw Audit data.

Sensitive identifiers should be protected or redacted where
appropriate.

Audit information must be visible only to authorized people.

============================================================
31. AUDIT INTEGRITY AND TAMPERING
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE:

SANOUR must be able to detect potential unauthorized alteration
of Audit records.

Cryptographic chaining or an equivalent integrity mechanism is
an architectural direction.

The exact algorithm, storage design, verification frequency,
and operational procedures remain technical decisions.

No absolute or perfect security guarantee is claimed.

Integrity verification results must be traceable.

Detection must not automatically repair, delete, or overwrite
suspected records.

============================================================
32. INTEGRITY INCIDENT RESPONSE
============================================================

When potential tampering or integrity failure is detected:

1. Record an INTEGRITY_ALERT.
2. Notify the security lead or authorized auditor.
3. Preserve suspected records and available evidence.
4. Do not silently delete or overwrite records.
5. Do not infer an actor or cause without evidence.
6. Do not automatically repair or correct the records.
7. Require evidence and authorization for remediation.

An integrity alert does not itself authorize an external effect,
retry, or automatic change of operation status.

============================================================
33. AUDIT CONTINUITY AND CAPACITY
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLES:

Required Audit Events must not be silently lost.

Temporary retention must be secure and bounded.

If capacity is exhausted:

- Do not silently delete required records.
- Do not silently discard critical events.
- Do not automatically redeliver.
- Preserve the actual registration and delivery state.
- Apply the admission requirements for the event's classification.

A Critical event must not be admitted unless a valid Audit Sink
registration confirmation is available.

The exact technical definition and mechanism for that confirmation
remain unresolved.

Temporary storage design, capacity limits, and full-capacity behavior
remain technical design matters.

============================================================
34. EVENT CLASSIFICATION
============================================================

Every event type must have a defined classification:

- CRITICAL
- IMPORTANT
- INFORMATIONAL

No event becomes Critical merely because an individual module
or Agent labels it Critical.

Classification changes require reason, approval, and traceable
history.

If an event is directly tied to admission of a financial or security
request, applicable Critical requirements must be applied.

Historical events must not be silently reclassified.

Classification history must remain traceable.

A change in classification does not authorize retry, redelivery,
or state transition.

------------------------------------------------------------
34.1 CRITICAL EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

OWNER-APPROVED LIST:

1. Admission of financial or accounting requests.
2. Registration of an external effect, such as payment or transfer.
3. A human decision to resolve UNKNOWN.
4. Issuance of authorization for a subsequent attempt or redelivery.
5. Changes to roles or security permissions.
6. Detection of tampering or integrity failure, including
   INTEGRITY_ALERT.

A Critical event must not be admitted without valid Audit Sink
registration confirmation.

The technical confirmation definition remains unresolved.

------------------------------------------------------------
34.2 IMPORTANT EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

OWNER-APPROVED LIST:

1. Non-critical system configuration changes.
2. Customer or supplier contact information changes.
3. Operational errors without financial-effect stoppage.
4. Reporting configuration changes.
5. User login and logout.
6. Non-sensitive management information changes.
7. Capacity and performance alerts.
8. Module version or configuration changes.

Important events must be handled according to the applicable
registration policy.

Their classification alone does not establish that every such event
must stop admission of an unrelated request.

The detailed admission behavior remains subject to policy design.

------------------------------------------------------------
34.3 INFORMATIONAL EVENTS
------------------------------------------------------------

OWNER-APPROVED LIST:

1. Viewing general reports.
2. Viewing statistical dashboards.
3. Producing periodic reports.
4. Resource usage and performance statistics.
5. General system notifications.
6. Non-sensitive diagnostic events.

Informational events must not be used as substitutes for required
Critical or Important records.

------------------------------------------------------------
34.4 CLASSIFICATION AUTHORITY AND REVIEW
------------------------------------------------------------

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLES:

Overall classification policy changes require approval from the
SANOUR architecture owner.

The security lead may propose changes but cannot approve them alone.

Lowering a Critical classification requires special review and
explicit owner approval.

Classification policy should be reviewed every six months.

An out-of-cycle review should occur after:

- A major security incident.
- A significant architecture change.
- Identification of a new material risk.

A review does not automatically change classifications.

The review result must be recorded even if no change is made.

============================================================
35. RETENTION AND RECORD PROTECTION
============================================================

Audit records must be retained according to an approved retention
policy.

No automatic deletion may occur without a valid retention policy.

Different categories of data may have different retention periods.

The following remain unresolved:

- Exact retention periods.
- Review intervals for each data category.
- Deletion conditions.
- Legal and jurisdictional requirements.
- Secure disposal procedures.

An Administrator role alone does not authorize silent deletion
or alteration of protected Audit history.

============================================================
36. POLICY CONFLICTS AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE
============================================================

Policy conflicts must be registered and referred to the authorized
governance authority.

No irreversible action should proceed while a material conflict
remains unresolved.

A conflict must not be used to bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

The decision record must identify:

- Reason for the conflict.
- Available evidence.
- Decision-maker.
- Decision time.
- Final decision and scope.

Emergency conditions do not automatically waive:

- Security requirements.
- Accounting integrity.
- Audit evidence preservation.
- Zero Automatic Retry.

If safe continuation is not possible, the affected operation must
stop and be escalated to a human authority.

There is no automatic emergency override.

============================================================
37. SAFE HALT
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLE:

Safe Halt should stop only the affected operation or module where
possible.

The system must preserve the current state and available evidence.

Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

Safe Halt must not automatically:

- Complete an unfinished operation.
- Repeat an external effect.
- Change UNKNOWN to FAILED.
- Release or reclaim a lease in a way that initiates another effect.
- Authorize redelivery or resubmission.

Priority order:

1. Prevent unintended or duplicate financial effects.
2. Preserve Audit evidence and history.
3. Contain security risk.
4. Preserve healthy independent services.
5. Recover only after review, evidence, and authorization.

============================================================
38. SAFE RESUME
============================================================

Safe Resume requires:

1. The cause of the halt to be identified.
2. Credible evidence that the relevant risk is resolved.
3. The affected operation's state to be assessed.
4. The risk of repeating an external effect to be addressed.
5. An authorized person to record the reason, time, and approval.

Resuming a service does not authorize repetition of an earlier
operation.

UNKNOWN remains subject to its separate evidence and governance
rules.

------------------------------------------------------------
38.1 RESUME AUTHORITY
------------------------------------------------------------

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLES:

- Financial risk: finance/accounting lead.
- Security risk: security lead.
- Audit/evidence risk: audit lead.
- Cross-domain or conflicting risks: architecture owner or a
  governance authority designated by the owner.

No authority may use Safe Resume to bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

------------------------------------------------------------
38.2 HALT AND RESUME CASE RECORD
------------------------------------------------------------

A stop/resume case must have an independent traceable case ID.

The record should include:

- Affected operation and module IDs.
- Reason for the halt.
- Halt time.
- Evidence and risk assessment.
- Reviewer and decision-maker.
- Decision to resume or remain stopped.
- Reason and time of the decision.
- Scope of authorization.
- Links to previous Audit records.

The case record must not replace original history or automatically
change UNKNOWN.

============================================================
39. FOUR-DATABASE TARGET ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION: D-02

The target topology consists of four physical databases:

1. Evidence Ledger DB.
2. Usage DB.
3. Governance DB.
4. Audit Sink DB.

This is an approved future design direction.

It is not evidence that the four-database architecture has already
been implemented.

The reviewed historical code state indicated:

- Usage and Governance shared one physical database.
- Audit Sink was separate by default.
- Evidence Ledger was not wired into the reviewed production runtime.

These historical observations must not be presented as current
production facts without fresh evidence.

The four-database target does not imply atomic transactions across
all four databases.

Separate transactions do not establish a single atomic transaction
across independent database boundaries.

Where Usage and Governance share a physical file, their observations
are not independent.

============================================================
40. D-03 — COORDINATED FOUR-DATABASE BACKUP
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION:

A coordinated backup set should contain four independent valid
database backups.

The backup set should include:

- One valid backup for each target database.
- Start time for each database backup.
- End time for each database backup.
- A central Manifest linking the four backup artifacts.

If any backup is incomplete or invalid, the overall set must not
be represented as a complete valid set.

The Manifest must not claim that all four databases represent the
same snapshot time.

The backup set does not establish cross-database atomicity.

Restore requires a separate decision and authorization.

This design direction does not authorize any backup, database
creation, database opening, migration, restore, or data operation.

============================================================
41. D-04 — READ-ONLY VERIFIER AND MIGRATION SEPARATION
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION:

Inspection and verification must be strictly read-only.

Migration must be performed by a separate tool or separately
controlled process.

The verifier must not silently:

- Create a database.
- Modify a schema.
- Stamp ownership.
- Adopt an unknown database.
- Migrate data.
- Repair records.
- Create or alter sidecar files through an inspection operation.

Migration requires separate authorization.

No verifier or migration implementation is authorized by this
architectural decision.

============================================================
42. RESERVATION ACCOUNTING
============================================================

OWNER-APPROVED PRINCIPLES:

Reservation and actual consumption are separate concepts.

A reservation is not consumption.

Actual usage must be based on reliable evidence of consumption.

A failed or UNKNOWN reservation must not automatically become
consumption.

Reservation release must be traceable.

Automatic correction or release must not corrupt Usage accounting.

The exact reservation lifecycle, reconciliation rules, and
implementation remain technical design matters.
