1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE
============================================================

This document defines the foundational safety, control,
ownership, emergency governance, audit, and succession
principles of the SANOUR platform.

SANOUR is intended to be a modular, provider-independent
AI platform. Its future capabilities may include:

- AI models and model providers.
- Autonomous and semi-autonomous Agents.
- Tools and external integrations.
- Memory and contextual services.
- APIs and SDKs.
- Multilingual and low-resource language capabilities.
- Offline and online operation.
- Future models and hardware.

This document establishes the boundaries within which
those capabilities may operate.

Its objectives are:

1. Prevent AI systems from controlling their own governance.
2. Prevent unauthorized privilege expansion.
3. Maintain independent safety controls.
4. Protect financial, accounting, security, and audit integrity.
5. Preserve legitimate user and administrator activity
   wherever safe.
6. Establish controlled emergency response.
7. Protect ownership and succession processes.
8. Maintain traceable and reviewable decisions.
9. Prevent unauthorized automatic repetition of external effects.
10. Separate architectural approval from implementation approval.

This document does not authorize implementation.

3. FOUNDATIONAL SAFETY PRINCIPLE
============================================================

APPROVED

No AI system, Agent, autonomous process, or external provider
may independently change, remove, weaken, disable, or bypass
SANOUR's independent control and safety mechanisms.

Final governance authority remains outside AI control.

Therefore:

1. AI cannot independently modify its foundational security
   policies.
2. AI cannot increase its own privileges without authorization.
3. AI cannot create Agents with greater privileges without
   authorization.
4. AI cannot disable or bypass independent safety controls.
5. Sensitive actions require valid authorization before execution.
6. An independent mechanism must be capable of restricting
   or stopping AI activities.
7. Restarting sensitive AI activities after a dangerous incident
   requires human review and appropriate authorization.

No absolute security guarantee is claimed.

Compromise of hardware, operating systems, credentials,
cryptographic keys, or independent control infrastructure
remains a possible risk.

4. SEPARATION OF AUTHORITY DOMAINS
============================================================

APPROVED AS AN ARCHITECTURAL DIRECTION

SANOUR separates three authority domains.

4.1 AI DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Reasoning and analysis.
- Processing authorized tasks.
- Using permitted tools.
- Producing recommendations and outputs.
- Operating within assigned permissions and limits.

The AI domain cannot govern or modify the independent
safety domain.

4.2 INDEPENDENT CONTROL AND SAFETY DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Authorization enforcement.
- Access control.
- Resource and execution limits.
- Restrictions on sensitive operations.
- Emergency stop and containment.
- Independent control of AI access to tools, providers,
  networks, and sensitive operations.

This domain must not depend on AI decisions for its own
security enforcement.

4.3 HUMAN GOVERNANCE DOMAIN

Responsible for:

- Approving foundational policies.
- Defining authorized roles.
- Approving exceptional permissions.
- Reviewing serious incidents.
- Approving foundational changes.
- Governing ownership and succession.

AI systems and Agents cannot replace this authority.

The precise technical separation between these domains
remains subject to technical design.

5. INDEPENDENT EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
============================================================

APPROVED AS AN ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENT

SANOUR must have an independent Emergency Safety Layer.

The layer must:

- Be independent of AI reasoning and Agent decisions.
- Have bounded and clearly defined authority.
- Control high-risk operations.
- Be capable of restricting AI access to sensitive tools,
  providers, networks, and operations.
- Be protected against unauthorized modification.
- Remain subject to human governance.

5.1 PROPORTIONAL CONTROL

Restrictions must apply to the smallest affected scope.

Preferred order:

1. Restrict the affected Agent.
2. Restrict the affected Provider or execution path.
3. Preserve healthy independent user services.
4. Stop a wider system only when systemic risk requires it.

Each restriction should record:

- Cause.
- Affected scope.
- Remaining available functions.
- Responsible authority.
- Start time.
- Review time.
- Conditions required to remove the restriction.

5.2 NORMAL USER ACCESS

APPROVED

Emergency controls must not unnecessarily prevent normal
users or legitimate administrators from using healthy services.

The safety layer controls AI and high-risk operations.
It must not unnecessarily block unrelated healthy user functions.

Security restrictions must be proportional and scope-limited.

5.3 FAILURE OF THE SAFETY LAYER

APPROVED

FAIL SAFE, NOT FAIL OPEN.

If the Emergency Safety Layer becomes unavailable:

1. Sensitive operations stop or remain restricted until
   valid control is restored.
2. Healthy independent low-risk services may continue
   where their safety can be established.
3. Agents cannot acquire new privileges or permissions.
4. Providers and external tools cannot initiate new sensitive
   operations without valid authorization.
5. Failure evidence and records must be preserved.
6. Resumption of sensitive operations requires review of
   control-layer health and approval by an authorized human.

5.4 AVOIDING A SINGLE POINT OF FAILURE

APPROVED AS AN ARCHITECTURAL OBJECTIVE

The Emergency Safety Layer should not become a single point
of failure.

The technical methods for redundancy, independent monitoring,
safe degradation, and recovery remain unresolved.

No automatic recovery or permission expansion is authorized.

6. ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL AND PROTECTION
============================================================

APPROVED

Administrative access does not equal unlimited authority.

1. An ordinary administrator cannot disable the Emergency
   Safety Layer.
2. Foundational safety-policy changes require approval from
   the architecture owner or an explicitly designated
   governance authority.
3. Highly sensitive operations should require approval from
   two independent people where feasible.
4. Important administrative decisions must be recorded.
5. No administrator or Agent may independently remove all
   safety protections.
6. Administrative privileges must be limited to defined duties.

The detailed role model, permission matrix, authentication
mechanisms, and approval workflow remain unresolved.

7. OWNER ACCOUNT PROTECTION
============================================================

APPROVED

The owner's account is a critical governance identity.

Required architectural protections:

1. Strong multi-factor authentication.
2. Re-authentication for critical changes.
3. Independent recovery keys stored securely and offline.
4. Security delays for highly sensitive changes, with
   an opportunity to cancel.
5. Traceable records of requests, approvals, and outcomes.
6. Suspicious critical changes suspended pending human review.
7. No unnecessary burden on ordinary user access.

Possession of ordinary account credentials alone must not
automatically grant unrestricted authority to change every
foundational safety mechanism.

Technical details of authentication, key custody, delay
periods, and detection remain unresolved.

8. EMERGENCY OWNER RECOVERY
============================================================

APPROVED

If the owner loses a password, authentication device,
or primary key:

1. Recovery must be independent of AI and Agent decisions.
2. Recovery keys must be securely maintained offline.
3. Identity recovery requires multiple independent forms
   of evidence or authentication factors.
4. Ordinary administrators and Agents cannot appoint
   a new owner.
5. Recovery steps must be recorded and auditable.
6. Critical authority cannot be transferred before identity
   verification is completed.

The detailed recovery procedure, evidence standards,
custody arrangements, and legal requirements remain unresolved.

9. EMERGENCY SUCCESSION
============================================================

APPROVED

Emergency succession is separate from permanent ownership.

9.1 DESIGNATION

The owner may designate an emergency successor or trustee
in advance.

9.2 LIMITED AUTHORITY

The successor's authority is limited to defined emergency
responsibilities required to preserve SANOUR's security
and continuity.

The emergency successor:

- Does not automatically become permanent owner.
- Cannot transfer permanent ownership.
- Cannot weaken foundational safety policies.
- Cannot bypass Zero Automatic Retry.
- Cannot appoint a new permanent owner.
- Cannot use emergency authority to expand personal privileges.

9.3 ACTIVATION

The owner's absence alone does not activate the successor.

Activation requires:

1. Verification of the owner's unavailability under
   predefined conditions.
2. Independent verification of the successor's identity.
3. Approval by an independent authorized human authority.

No Agent may activate, cancel, or modify the successor's mandate.

9.4 TERMINATION

Ending the emergency mandate also requires independent
human approval.

The owner's return does not automatically erase records
or silently transfer authority.

The beginning, scope, decisions, and end of the mandate
must remain traceable.

The detailed activation and termination workflow remains
subject to technical and governance design.


============================================================
10. PERMANENT OWNERSHIP SUCCESSION
============================================================

APPROVED

Permanent ownership transfer is distinct from emergency
succession.

1. An emergency successor does not automatically become
   permanent owner.
2. Permanent transfer requires a valid succession document
   established and approved in advance by the owner.
3. Its validity must be assessed using independent evidence.
4. If no valid document exists, transfer stops.
5. If a material dispute exists, transfer stops pending
   proper resolution.
6. AI systems, Agents, and ordinary administrators cannot
   appoint a new permanent owner.
7. Applicable laws and legal rights must be respected.

Technical possession of an account, device, key, or server
does not itself establish ownership.

11. INDEPENDENT PERMANENT SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
============================================================

APPROVED AS AN ARCHITECTURAL DIRECTION

A separate authority shall review and approve permanent
ownership succession.

It must be independent of:

- AI systems and Agents.
- Ordinary system administration.
- Any individual applicant seeking ownership.

Its responsibilities:

1. Review the succession document.
2. Review identity and legal evidence.
3. Identify conflicts and disputed claims.
4. Suspend transfer when evidence is insufficient.
5. Record its review and decision.
6. Maintain an auditable record.

Approval of succession does not automatically grant
permission to modify foundational safety policies.

Its legal status, jurisdiction, and formal constitution
require separate legal and governance design.


============================================================
12. SUCCESSION AUTHORITY MEMBERSHIP
============================================================

APPROVED

Structure:

- Three principal members.
- Two designated alternate members.

12.1 APPOINTMENT

The owner appoints the initial members.

Alternates are designated in advance.

AI systems and Agents cannot select, appoint, or dismiss members.

12.2 VOTING

Permanent ownership transfer requires at least two
affirmative votes from three valid and eligible members.

No single member can approve a transfer independently.

A member with a conflict of interest cannot vote on
the affected case.

If enough valid and independent members are unavailable,
the transfer stops.

12.3 INDEPENDENCE

Multiple accounts alone do not establish independent approval.

Members must have:

- Independently verifiable identities.
- Independent authentication methods.
- Separate individual credentials.
- No shared approval credentials.
- A duty to disclose conflicts of interest.

No Agent may cast a vote or approve a decision for a member.

Evidence of collusion or material conflict requires
suspension and human review.


============================================================
13. MEMBER QUALIFICATION
============================================================

APPROVED

Members must:

1. Have a verifiable real identity.
2. Declare independence and relevant conflicts.
3. Understand their legal and security responsibilities.
4. Maintain independent accounts and authentication methods.
5. Never delegate their vote to an Agent or another person.
6. Remain qualified throughout their appointment.

Family, friendship, or professional relationships alone
do not automatically establish or disqualify eligibility.

Actual independence and case-specific conflicts must be assessed.

Loss of qualification requires replacement through
the recorded governance process.


============================================================
14. RESIGNATION, REMOVAL AND REPLACEMENT
============================================================

APPROVED

1. A member may formally resign.
2. Death, incapacity, or loss of qualification may trigger
   replacement review.
3. Removal requires documented grounds and an independent
   procedure.
4. No member may unilaterally remove another member.
5. A member under review cannot participate in the sensitive
   decision affected by that review.
6. Membership changes must be recorded.
7. Historical decisions and records must be preserved.
8. If enough qualified members are unavailable, permanent
   transfer remains suspended.

14.1 ALTERNATE MEMBERS

An alternate initially completes only the remaining term
of the replaced principal member.

Entry requires formal recording and renewed qualification review.

Any subsequent independent appointment begins a new
five-year term only after valid selection.

Replacement must not create automatic or unlimited
extensions of authority.


============================================================
15. TERMS OF APPOINTMENT
============================================================

APPROVED

- Principal member term: five years.
- One renewal may be permitted after independent evaluation.
- Qualification and conflict review: annually.
- Alternate members must maintain their qualifications.

Expiry of a term does not create new authority.

If enough valid members are unavailable, the authority
cannot approve permanent ownership transfer until a valid
composition is restored.

The renewal and replacement evaluation procedure remains
subject to further governance documentation.


============================================================
16. DISAGREEMENT AND CONFLICT RESOLUTION
============================================================

APPROVED

If members disagree on a sensitive decision:

1. No member may independently execute the decision.
2. The request remains suspended until properly resolved.
3. The matter is referred to a higher authorized governance
   authority.
4. Evidence, objections, and reasons from each side are preserved.
5. Silence or passage of time is not approval.
6. If disagreement remains unresolved, the sensitive operation
   does not proceed.
7. Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

No AI system or Agent may independently select a winning
position in a governance disagreement.

17. ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY
============================================================

APPROVED

The initial authorized logical effect is the first attempt,
admitted under existing admission and governance rules.

A subsequent attempt is any further external write or delivery
for the same logical effect, identified by effect_type and
logical_key.

System-initiated subsequent attempts are prohibited.

A subsequent attempt requires its own specific, recorded,
per-instance authorization under separate governance.

This includes:

- Financial and other external effects.
- Provider requests.
- Audit Sink delivery.
- Lease expiry and reclaim paths.
- Startup replay.
- Redelivery and resubmission.

UNKNOWN never automatically becomes failure or permission
to resend.

A late result from the original worker is not itself a new attempt.

Idempotency does not authorize another attempt.

No emergency, succession, administrative, or ownership
mechanism may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

The authorization process, authority, storage, and enforcement
mechanism remain unresolved.

18. AUDIT EVENT IDENTITY AND DELIVERY
============================================================

APPROVED

18.1 AUDIT EVENT IDENTITY

Each internal Audit event has its own independent logical identity.

The approved conceptual distinction is:

- audit_event_id: identity of the internal Audit event.
- outbox_key: delivery identity.
- payload_digest: payload integrity reference.
- logical effect identity: identity of the underlying external effect.

Redelivery does not create a new logical Audit event.

These identities must not be conflated.

The technical relationship and enforcement mechanisms remain
unresolved.

18.2 AUDIT SINK DELIVERY

Zero Automatic Retry applies to Audit Sink delivery.

No automatic redelivery is authorized.

UNKNOWN delivery state remains UNKNOWN.

Lease expiry and startup replay do not authorize redelivery.

Every subsequent Audit delivery requires its own specific,
recorded, per-event/per-attempt human authorization.

Audit delivery authorization does not authorize repetition
of the original external effect.

Existing S3-2 mechanisms are not authorized to be modified,
disabled, or operated by this document.

18.3 CLAIM AND SEND

A Claim alone is not Send authorization.

Send requires independent valid authorization for the same
event and attempt.

Claim must not bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

Whether Claim without Send is permitted remains unresolved.

18.4 HISTORICAL S3 REFERENCES

S3-2:
Automatic Audit Sink redelivery is prohibited by the approved
architectural decision. No implementation change is authorized.

S3-3:
Claim/Send behavior remains unresolved. Claim-only permission
must not be assumed.

S3-4:
Historical behavior remains UNVERIFIED until credible source
evidence is available.

No source behavior may be invented or changed by assumption.

19. UNKNOWN, EVIDENCE AND HUMAN AUTHORITY
============================================================

APPROVED

UNKNOWN must not automatically be converted into failure.

A final determination requires credible, traceable evidence
linked to the exact operation.

Evidence assessment must consider:

1. Identity of the operation.
2. Source of the evidence.
3. Authenticity.
4. Integrity.
5. Time and provenance.

Timeout, lease expiry, missing response, missing receipt,
or missing record alone is insufficient proof of failure.

Provider acceptance is not necessarily proof of completed effect.

Reporting must distinguish:

- ACCEPTED
- COMPLETED
- FAILED
- UNKNOWN

These labels do not establish an approved operational
state machine or automatic transition mechanism.

Final UNKNOWN determination requires an authorized human.

The record must include:

- Approver.
- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Time.

Human determination alone does not authorize resubmission.

Corrections and new determinations must be recorded as
separate traceable events, not silent overwrites.

20. READ-ONLY STATUS AND HISTORY
============================================================

APPROVED ARCHITECTURAL DIRECTION

20.1 ACCESS SCOPE

Read-only composite status is intended for internal
authorized operators.

20.2 COMPOSITE STATUS

The status view covers the four target database domains.

For each reported field, it should show:

- Authoritative source.
- Source database.
- Last observation time.
- Completeness.
- Availability of the relevant database.
- Staleness where applicable.
- Disagreement among sources.
- UNKNOWN where the outcome remains unknown.

20.3 NO INFERENCE

The status view must never infer success or failure from
missing, stale, unavailable, or inconsistent information.

If a database is unavailable, it must be shown as unavailable.

Information from the remaining databases must not be used
to invent the missing database's state.

If sources disagree, show each report separately and mark
the composite result INCONSISTENT.

A newer timestamp alone does not automatically make a source
the winner.

Resolution requires review and credible evidence.

20.4 STRICT READ-ONLY BOUNDARY

The status function does not authorize:

- Writes.
- State transitions.
- Recovery.
- Repair.
- Lease reclaim.
- Redelivery.
- Resubmission.
- Provider refresh.
- Automatic correction.

20.5 OPERATION HISTORY

History should preserve, where available:

- Initial request registration time.
- Attempt-start time.
- Provider response, if present.
- Audit event registration time.
- Last observation time.
- Human UNKNOWN determination.
- Decision reason and evidence.
- Source and time of each event.

History must not be filled with guesses or silently overwritten.

20.6 ACCESS ROLES

The proposed minimum access model is:

Viewer:
May view permitted information.

Auditor:
May view permitted information and audit history.

Administrator:
May view overall status and management reports.

These observation roles do not grant permission to change,
retry, repair, or redeliver operations.

Detailed role definitions and permissions remain unresolved.

21. AUDIT ACCESS, CONFIDENTIALITY AND INTEGRITY
============================================================

APPROVED ARCHITECTURAL REQUIREMENTS

21.1 ACCESS AUDIT

Access to sensitive information must itself be auditable.

Where applicable, record:

- User identity.
- Time.
- Data type.
- Operation ID.
- Allow or deny result.

Access logs must not contain unnecessary sensitive information.

Access logging does not change operation status.

21.2 CONFIDENTIALITY

Audit information must be visible only to appropriately
authorized people.

API keys, passwords, and raw authentication secrets must
never be stored in audit records.

Unnecessary sensitive user data must be avoided.

Secure identifiers or redacted information should be used
where appropriate.

21.3 INTEGRITY

SANOUR must be designed to detect potential tampering
of audit records.

Cryptographic chaining or an equivalent integrity mechanism
is an approved architectural direction.

Algorithm, storage, key management, and verification details
remain unresolved.

No absolute or unbreakable security guarantee is claimed.

Integrity-check results must be traceable.

Detection must not automatically repair or delete records.

21.4 INTEGRITY INCIDENT

When potential tampering is detected:

1. Record INTEGRITY_ALERT.
2. Notify the security lead or authorized auditor.
3. Preserve suspect records.
4. Do not silently delete or overwrite them.
5. Do not infer actor or cause without evidence.
6. Do not automatically repair or correct the records.
7. Review and remediation require evidence and appropriate
   authorization.

22. AUDIT CONTINUITY AND EVENT CLASSIFICATION
============================================================

APPROVED ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

22.1 AUDIT CONTINUITY

Required audit events must not be silently lost.

Admission-critical requests must not be accepted until
valid Audit Sink registration confirmation is obtained.

If registration is unavailable or unknown, the system must
report that status honestly.

No automatic redelivery is authorized.

Temporary retention must be secure and bounded.

If temporary capacity is full, records must not be silently
deleted.

The temporary retention design, capacity, duration, and
full-capacity behavior remain subject to technical design.

22.2 EVENT LEVELS

Every event type must have a defined level.

The approved levels are:

CRITICAL:
If valid registration confirmation is absent, the related
request is not admitted.

IMPORTANT:
The event must be registered. Its admission policy is
defined separately.

INFORMATIONAL:
Used for reporting and analytics. It cannot substitute
for Critical or Important registration.

An event level does not authorize retry or state changes.

22.3 APPROVED CRITICAL EVENT LIST

1. Admission of financial/accounting requests.
2. Registration of external effects such as payments
   and transfers.
3. Human decisions resolving UNKNOWN.
4. Issuance of authorization for a subsequent attempt
   or Audit Sink redelivery.
5. Changes to roles and security permissions.
6. Detection of tampering or integrity failure
   (INTEGRITY_ALERT).

No event is Critical merely by assumption.

22.4 APPROVED IMPORTANT EVENT LIST

1. Non-critical system configuration changes.
2. Customer or supplier contact information changes.
3. Operational errors without financial effect stoppage.
4. Reporting configuration changes.
5. User login and logout.
6. Non-sensitive management information changes.
7. Capacity and performance alerts.
8. Module version or configuration changes.

22.5 APPROVED INFORMATIONAL EVENT LIST

1. Viewing general reports.
2. Viewing statistical dashboards.
3. Producing periodic reports.
4. Resource usage and performance statistics.
5. General system notifications.
6. Non-sensitive diagnostic events.

22.6 CLASSIFICATION CHANGES

Changing an event's level requires a reason and approval
from the authorized authority.

If an event becomes tied to admission of a financial or
security-sensitive request, Critical requirements apply.

Classification changes must not overwrite historical events.

Classification history must remain traceable.

22.7 CLASSIFICATION AUTHORITY

Overall classification policy changes require approval
from the SANOUR architecture owner.

The security lead may propose a change but cannot approve
it alone.

An approved change records:

- Reason.
- Effective date.
- Policy version.

Lowering Critical to Important or Informational requires
special review and explicit architecture-owner approval.

Periodic review occurs every six months.

An out-of-cycle review is required after a major security
event, architecture change, or newly identified risk.

A review alone does not change classifications.

The review result must be logged even when no change occurs.

23. POLICY CONFLICTS AND EMERGENCY RULES
============================================================

APPROVED

The system must not arbitrarily choose between conflicting
policies or requirements.

A conflict must be:

- Registered.
- Traceable.
- Referred to an authorized authority.

No irreversible action may proceed until the conflict
is resolved.

Policy resolution must not bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

The decision record must include:

- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Decision-maker.
- Time.

23.1 EMERGENCY

Security, accounting integrity, audit evidence preservation,
and Zero Automatic Retry are not waived during an emergency.

If safe continuation is impossible:

- Stop the affected operation.
- Preserve relevant evidence.
- Escalate to a human.

No automatic emergency override is authorized.

24. SAFE HALT AND SAFE RESUME
============================================================

APPROVED

24.1 SAFE HALT

Stop only the affected operation or module when safe isolation
is possible.

Healthy independent services may continue.

Preserve state and evidence.

Do not automatically complete or repeat a partially finished
transaction.

Resume only after review, evidence, and appropriate permission.

Do not automatically change UNKNOWN.

24.2 SAFE HALT PRIORITY

1. Prevent unintended or duplicate financial effects.
2. Preserve audit evidence and history.
3. Contain security risk.
4. Preserve healthy independent services.
5. Recover only after review, evidence, and authorization.

24.3 SAFE RESUME CONDITIONS

Before resuming an affected sensitive operation:

- The cause must be identified.
- Credible evidence must support risk resolution.
- The affected operation's status must be assessed.
- The risk of repeated financial effect or unintended send
  must be addressed.
- An authorized person must record reason, time, and approval.

Resume does not authorize repetition of an old operation.

UNKNOWN remains subject to its own policy.

24.4 RESUME AUTHORITY

The approved responsibility direction is:

Financial/accounting risk:
Authorized finance or accounting lead.

Security risk:
Security lead.

Audit/evidence integrity risk:
Audit lead.

Cross-domain or conflicting authority:
Architecture owner or governance authority designated
by the owner.

None may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

24.5 CASE RECORD

A Safe Halt/Resume case should have:

- Independent traceable case ID.
- Affected operation and module IDs.
- Reason and stop time.
- Evidence and risk assessment.
- Reviewer or decision-maker.
- Resume or continued-stop decision.
- Reason, time, and scope of authorization.
- Links to prior audit records.

This case record must not replace original operation history
or automatically change UNKNOWN.

25. FOUR-DATABASE ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

The target architecture consists of four separate database
domains:

1. Evidence Ledger DB.
2. Usage DB.
3. Governance DB.
4. Audit Sink DB.

This is an approved architectural target.

It does not prove that the four databases currently exist
as four independent physical databases.

It does not authorize their creation, opening, migration,
schema modification, or operational integration.

25.1 ATOMICITY

Atomicity across four independent database boundaries is
not guaranteed by the current architecture.

Separate transactions, even when databases share a physical
file, do not by themselves establish a single atomic
transaction across subsystems.

Where Usage and Governance share a physical file,
their observations are not independent.

No composite status view may claim global atomicity.

25.2 COORDINATED BACKUP DIRECTION

The approved design direction for a coordinated backup set:

- Four independent valid backups.
- Start and end time recorded for each database.
- A central Manifest links the four backups as one set.
- If any backup is incomplete or invalid, the set is not
  considered complete and valid.
- No claim of identical snapshot time or global atomicity.
- Restore requires separate decision and authorization.

This direction does not authorize any actual backup,
database opening, creation, restore, or migration.

25.3 READ-ONLY VERIFIER AND MIGRATION

Inspection must be strictly read-only.

Migration must be performed by a separate migration tool.

A verifier must not create, modify, migrate, stamp, adopt,
or otherwise alter databases.

Migration requires separate authorization.

This is an architectural direction, not implementation approval.

APPROVED ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

Reservations must be distinguished from actual consumption.

1. Reserve does not mean consume.
2. Actual usage must be based on reliable evidence.
3. Failed or UNKNOWN reservation does not automatically
   become consumption.
4. Release must be traceable.
5. Automatic correction or release must not corrupt Usage.

Detailed reservation lifecycle, reconciliation, and
technical enforcement remain unresolved.

27. RETENTION AND RECORD PROTECTION
============================================================

APPROVED ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLES

Audit and governance records must be retained according
to an approved retention policy.

1. No automatic deletion without an approved policy.
2. Retention periods need not be identical for all data.
3. Original records must not be silently overwritten.
