================================================================
1. FOUNDATIONAL SAFETY PRINCIPLE
================================================================

APPROVED

No AI system, Agent, autonomous process, or AI-controlled component
may have authority to change, remove, weaken, or bypass SANOUR's
independent control and safety mechanisms.

Final governance authority must remain outside AI control.

No AI component may:

- Change its own foundational security policies.
- Grant itself additional privileges.
- Create an Agent with greater privileges without authorization.
- Disable or bypass independent safety controls.
- Appoint or replace the system owner.
- Change ownership succession rules.
- Authorize its own sensitive operations.
- Decide to ignore a human governance restriction.
- Remove or weaken Zero Automatic Retry.
- Authorize its own restart after a dangerous incident.

AI assistance, recommendations, planning, and execution must remain
within explicitly authorized boundaries.

An AI component cannot use its own output as authorization.

================================================================
2. AUTHORITY DOMAINS
================================================================

PROPOSED ARCHITECTURAL MODEL

The following separation is proposed for SANOUR:

A. AI DOMAIN
   - Models
   - Agents
   - Planning and reasoning
   - Tools and task execution
   - User-facing AI services

B. INDEPENDENT CONTROL AND SAFETY DOMAIN
   - Safety enforcement
   - Permission validation
   - Privilege boundaries
   - Sensitive-operation restrictions
   - Emergency limitation and stop controls
   - Safety-state monitoring

C. HUMAN GOVERNANCE DOMAIN
   - Owner authority
   - Approved governance authorities
   - Ownership and succession decisions
   - Foundational policy approval
   - Human authorization for sensitive decisions

The AI Domain must not control the independent safety mechanisms
or the final human governance authority.

The exact technical boundaries, deployment topology, and
independence mechanisms remain PROPOSED.

================================================================
3. INDEPENDENT EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

SANOUR must have independent safety controls capable of limiting
or stopping AI access to sensitive tools, network paths, and
operations.

No AI Agent may disable, weaken, or bypass these controls.

The safety architecture must support:

- Independent restriction of sensitive operations.
- Human-authorized recovery.
- Preservation of evidence.
- Protection against privilege escalation.
- Proportional containment.
- Continued operation of healthy independent services where safe.

PROPOSED TECHNICAL CHARACTERISTICS

The Emergency Safety Layer should be:

- Independent of AI decision-making.
- Bounded in authority.
- Small and understandable where practical.
- Protected against unauthorized modification.
- Designed to avoid a single point of failure.

The exact implementation, hardware, process isolation, and
redundancy model remain unresolved.

================================================================
4. PROPORTIONAL SAFETY AND NORMAL USER ACCESS
================================================================

APPROVED

Safety controls must focus on AI and high-risk operations without
unnecessarily blocking ordinary users or healthy services.

Restrictions should affect the smallest safe scope.

Preferred containment order:

1. Affected Agent.
2. Affected Provider or execution path.
3. Affected sensitive operation or module.
4. Wider system restriction only when the risk requires it.

A restriction record should identify:

- Cause.
- Affected scope.
- Start time.
- Remaining permitted functions.
- Responsible authority.
- Review conditions.
- Conditions for lifting the restriction.

The system must not claim that an unaffected service is stopped
when it remains safely operational.

================================================================
5. FAIL SAFE, NOT FAIL OPEN
================================================================

APPROVED

If independent safety controls fail:

- Sensitive operations must stop or remain restricted.
- Agents must not acquire new privileges.
- Providers and tools must not perform new sensitive operations
  without valid authorization.
- Evidence must be preserved.
- Healthy independent low-risk services may continue if safe.
- Sensitive operations may resume only after review and
  appropriate human authorization.

Safety-control failure must never automatically grant additional
permissions.

Avoiding a single point of failure is an architectural objective.

================================================================
6. ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

An ordinary administrator cannot disable the Emergency Safety
Layer.

An administrator cannot independently:

- Remove foundational safety controls.
- Weaken Zero Automatic Retry.
- Transfer permanent ownership.
- Appoint a new owner.
- Authorize an AI system to bypass its safety boundaries.

Foundational safety-policy changes require owner or
owner-designated governance approval.

Highly sensitive operations should require two independent human
approvals where feasible.

Important administrative decisions must be recorded.

Detailed roles, authentication methods, and permission matrices
remain PROPOSED or UNRESOLVED until separately specified.

================================================================
7. OWNER ACCOUNT PROTECTION
================================================================

APPROVED

Owner-account protection must include:

- Strong multi-factor authentication.
- Re-authentication for critical changes.
- Independent offline recovery keys.
- Security delays for highly sensitive changes, with an
  opportunity to cancel.
- Recording of requests, approvals, and outcomes.
- Suspension of suspicious critical changes pending human review.

Protection must not create unnecessary friction for ordinary
user access.

AI and Agents cannot modify owner-account protection rules.

================================================================
8. EMERGENCY OWNER RECOVERY
================================================================

APPROVED

Emergency owner recovery must be independent of AI and Agents.

It must include:

- Secure offline recovery keys.
- Multiple independent verification factors.
- Verifiable evidence of the recovery request.
- Protection against ordinary administrator or Agent takeover.
- Traceable records of the recovery process.

No critical ownership transfer may proceed until verification
requirements are satisfied.

A recovery process must not permit an AI Agent to appoint a new
owner.

================================================================
9. EMERGENCY SUCCESSION
================================================================

APPROVED

The owner may designate an emergency successor or trustee in
advance.

Emergency succession authority is limited.

It does not automatically grant:

- Permanent ownership.
- Permanent owner appointment authority.
- Authority to weaken foundational safety policies.
- Authority to bypass Zero Automatic Retry.
- Unlimited privilege expansion.

Activation requires:

1. Verification of owner unavailability under predefined
   conditions.
2. Independent verification of the successor's identity.
3. Independent human approval.

Termination also requires independent human approval.

The owner's return does not automatically erase or transfer
previously recorded emergency decisions.

AI cannot activate, cancel, or modify emergency succession.

================================================================
10. PERMANENT OWNERSHIP TRANSFER
================================================================

APPROVED

Emergency succession does not automatically become permanent
ownership.

Permanent ownership transfer requires:

- A valid, pre-approved succession document.
- Independent evidence.
- Verification of the applicable legal requirements.
- Review by the independent succession authority.

If the required document is missing, invalid, or disputed,
the transfer must stop.

AI systems, Agents, and ordinary administrators cannot appoint
a permanent owner.

Applicable law must be followed.

================================================================
11. INDEPENDENT PERMANENT SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

The permanent succession authority must be independent of:

- AI systems and Agents.
- Ordinary administrators.
- The person applying for ownership transfer.

Its responsibilities include:

- Reviewing succession documents.
- Reviewing legal and identity evidence.
- Stopping a transfer when evidence is insufficient or disputed.
- Recording its decision and supporting evidence.

Succession authority does not automatically grant permission
to change foundational safety policies.

Legal constitution, jurisdiction, and formal establishment
remain UNRESOLVED.

================================================================
12. SUCCESSION AUTHORITY MEMBERSHIP
================================================================

APPROVED

Membership structure:

- Three principal members.
- Two designated alternate members.

The owner appoints the initial members.

AI systems have no role in selecting, dismissing, or replacing
members.

Permanent ownership transfer requires at least two affirmative
votes from three valid and eligible principal members.

No single member can independently approve a transfer.

A member with a relevant conflict of interest cannot vote on
the affected matter.

If fewer than the required valid members are available,
the transfer must stop.

Independent identity verification and authentication are
required. Multiple accounts alone do not establish independent
identity.

Shared credentials are prohibited.

Agents cannot vote.

Credible collusion concerns require suspension and independent
human review.

================================================================
13. MEMBER QUALIFICATION
================================================================

APPROVED

Members must:

- Have verifiable real identities.
- Declare relevant conflicts of interest.
- Demonstrate understanding of their legal and security duties.
- Use independent accounts and authentication.
- Maintain their qualifications.
- Avoid delegating their vote to an Agent or another person.

Family, friendship, or professional relationships alone neither
qualify nor disqualify a person.

Actual independence and conflicts must be assessed.

================================================================
14. RESIGNATION, REMOVAL, AND REPLACEMENT
================================================================

APPROVED

Resignation must be formally recorded.

Death, incapacity, or loss of qualification triggers review.

Removal requires documented grounds and an independent process.

No member may unilaterally remove another member.

A member under review is excluded from the affected sensitive
decision.

Membership changes must be recorded while preserving previous
records.

If the number of valid members is insufficient, permanent
ownership transfer stops.

An alternate first completes the remaining term.

A new independent five-year term may begin only after valid
selection.

================================================================
15. MEMBER TERMS
================================================================

APPROVED

Principal member term: Five years.

One renewal is permitted after independent evaluation.

Qualification and conflict-of-interest reviews occur annually.

Alternates must maintain their qualifications.

Expiry does not automatically create new authority.

If valid membership is incomplete, a sensitive transfer cannot
proceed.

================================================================
16. GOVERNANCE DISAGREEMENT
================================================================

APPROVED

No member may independently execute a disputed permanent
ownership transfer.

The request is suspended pending resolution.

The process must:

- Preserve evidence.
- Preserve objections and reasons.
- Escalate to the appropriate higher governance authority.
- Record decisions and responsible persons.

Silence or passage of time is not approval.

Unresolved disagreement means the sensitive operation does not
proceed.

Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

AI cannot select the winning position or resolve the dispute
by itself.

================================================================
17. ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY
================================================================

APPROVED — ZR-DEF

The initial authorized logical effect or request is the first
attempt, admitted under existing admission and governance rules.

A subsequent attempt is any further external write or delivery
for the same logical effect:

effect_type + logical_key

System-initiated subsequent attempts are prohibited.

This includes subsequent attempts initiated by:

- Timers.
- Schedulers.
- Startup replay.
- Lease expiry.
- Automatic redelivery.
- Automatic recovery.
- Background workers.
- Automatic resubmission.

A timer or scheduler remains automatic even if an operator
configured it.

A human-triggered subsequent attempt is not automatically
authorized.

Each subsequent attempt requires its own specific, recorded,
per-instance authorization under separate governance.

The following do not authorize another attempt:

- Idempotency.
- An unchanged idempotency key.
- Lease expiry.
- Missing response.
- Timeout.
- UNKNOWN status.
- A human's determination that an earlier operation failed,
  without separate authorization for the next attempt.

A late result from the original worker is not a new attempt.

This rule applies to original external effects and Audit Sink
delivery.

No exception is implied.

================================================================
18. AUDIT EVENT IDENTITY AND DELIVERY
================================================================

APPROVED — P3-A, P3-B, P3-C

Each internal Audit event has an independent durable:

audit_event_id

The following identities must remain distinct:

- audit_event_id: identity of the internal Audit event.
- outbox_key: delivery identity.
- payload_digest: payload integrity reference.
- effect identity: identity of the external logical effect.

Redelivery does not create a new logical Audit event.

Audit Sink delivery state is separate from the Audit event's
logical status.

Automatic Audit Sink redelivery is prohibited, including
redelivery caused by lease expiry or startup replay.

UNKNOWN delivery state remains UNKNOWN.

Each subsequent Audit delivery requires a specific, recorded,
per-event/per-attempt human authorization.

The record must include:

- Approver.
- Authorization time.
- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Event identity.
- Attempt identity.

Authorization is not reusable for a later attempt.

Audit redelivery authorization does not authorize repetition
of the original external effect.

No modification, disabling, or operation of existing S3-2
mechanisms is authorized by these decisions.

================================================================
19. UNKNOWN AND EVIDENCE GOVERNANCE
================================================================

APPROVED

UNKNOWN must not automatically become FAILED.

Evidence must be credible, traceable, and linked to the exact
operation.

Evidence assessment includes:

- Operation identity.
- Source.
- Authenticity.
- Integrity.
- Time and provenance.

The following alone are not proof of failure:

- Timeout.
- Lease expiry.
- Missing provider response.
- Missing receipt.
- Missing local record.
- Database unavailability.

Provider acceptance is not necessarily proof of completed
external effect.

The system must distinguish:

ACCEPTED
COMPLETED
FAILED
UNKNOWN

Final UNKNOWN determination belongs to an authorized human
using credible evidence.

The decision must record:

- Approver.
- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Decision time.

A human decision about UNKNOWN does not itself authorize
resubmission.

Audit history must not be silently overwritten or deleted.
Corrections and new determinations must be separate traceable
events.

20. R-2A: READ-ONLY STATUS AND HISTORY
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

Purpose:
Provide internal authorized operators with a read-only view of
operation status and history across the four target databases.

The status view must not perform:

- Writes.
- State transitions.
- Recovery.
- Repair.
- Lease reclaim.
- Redelivery.
- Resubmission.
- Provider refresh.
- Automatic correction.

20.1 PER-FIELD SOURCE AUTHORITY

For every displayed field, the system must identify:

- Authoritative source.
- Source database.
- Last observation timestamp.
- Completeness.
- Availability.
- Freshness.

A stale observation must never be presented as live.

An inaccessible database must be shown as unavailable.

The system must not infer missing information from the remaining
three databases.

20.2 INCONSISTENT INFORMATION

If sources disagree:

- Show each source's information separately.
- Mark the composite result INCONSISTENT.
- Do not automatically select a winning source.
- Do not select the newest timestamp as the winner merely
  because it is newer.
- Require separate review and evidence for adjudication.

20.3 OPERATION INFORMATION

Where available, the view should include:

- Unique operation ID.
- Reported status.
- Source database for each field.
- Last observation time.
- Completeness.
- Disagreement among sources.
- Availability of each database.
- UNKNOWN status without automatic interpretation.

20.4 TRACEABLE HISTORY

The view should display separately:

- Initial request registration time.
- Attempt-start time.
- Provider response, if present.
- Audit event registration time.
- Last observation time.
- Human UNKNOWN decision.
- Decision reason and evidence.

Every event must identify its source and time.

No inferred event may be displayed as a confirmed event.

20.5 ROLE-BASED ACCESS

Approved role direction:

Viewer:
Permitted viewing information only.

Auditor:
Permitted information and audit history.

Administrator:
Permitted overall status and management reports.

These roles do not acquire permission to change, retry,
repair, redeliver, or recover operations merely by viewing them.

Detailed authentication, role definitions, and permission
matrices remain subject to separate design.

20.6 ACCESS AUDIT

Access to sensitive information must itself be auditable.

Record:

- User ID.
- Access time.
- Data type.
- Operation ID where applicable.
- Allow or deny result.

Avoid placing unnecessary sensitive data in access logs.

Access logging does not change the operation's status.

================================================================
21. AUDIT CONFIDENTIALITY AND INTEGRITY
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

Confidentiality:

- Audit information is available only to authorized persons.
- API keys, passwords, and authentication secrets must never
  be stored raw in Audit records.
- Unnecessary sensitive information must be avoided.
- Secure identifiers and redaction should be used where needed.
- Access to sensitive Audit information is itself auditable.

Integrity:

SANOUR must be able to detect potential unauthorized
modification or deletion of Audit records.

Cryptographic chaining or an equivalent integrity mechanism
is an approved architectural direction.

The exact algorithm, key management, storage arrangement,
and verification implementation remain unresolved.

Integrity checks must produce traceable results.

Detection does not automatically authorize repair or deletion.

22. AUDIT EVENT CLASSIFICATION
================================================================

APPROVED

Every event type must have a defined classification:

CRITICAL
IMPORTANT
INFORMATIONAL

No event is automatically Critical merely because it is
important to a particular module.

22.1 CRITICAL EVENTS

The approved Critical list is:

1. Admission of financial/accounting requests.
2. Registration of an external effect, such as payment or
   transfer.
3. Human decision to resolve UNKNOWN.
4. Issuance of authorization for a subsequent attempt or
   redelivery.
5. Changes to roles or security permissions.
6. Detection of tampering or integrity failure
   (INTEGRITY_ALERT).

If valid Audit Sink registration confirmation is absent for
a Critical event, the related request must not be admitted.

22.2 IMPORTANT EVENTS

The approved Important list is:

1. Non-critical system configuration changes.
2. Customer or supplier contact information changes.
3. Operational errors without financial-effect stoppage.
4. Reporting configuration changes.
5. User login and logout.
6. Non-sensitive management information changes.
7. Capacity and performance alerts.
8. Module version or configuration changes.

22.3 INFORMATIONAL EVENTS

The approved Informational list is:

1. Viewing general reports.
2. Viewing statistical dashboards.
3. Producing periodic reports.
4. Resource usage and performance statistics.
5. General system notifications.
6. Non-sensitive diagnostic events.

22.4 CLASSIFICATION GOVERNANCE

Every event type must have a defined level.

Changing a classification requires a reason and approval.

If an event is tied to admission of a financial or security
request, Critical requirements apply.

Historical events must not be overwritten.

Classification history must remain traceable.

Overall policy changes require approval by the SANOUR
architecture owner.

A security lead may propose a change but cannot approve it
alone.

The record must include:

- Reason.
- Effective date.
- Policy version.
- Approving authority.

Lowering a Critical classification requires special review
and explicit owner approval.

Review occurs every six months and additionally after a major
security incident, architecture change, or newly identified risk.

A review does not itself change event classifications.

The review outcome must be recorded even when no change is made.

================================================================
23. AUDIT CONTINUITY AND CAPACITY
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

Required Audit events must not be silently lost.

The system should use secure, bounded temporary retention
when normal Audit registration is unavailable.

If temporary capacity is full:

- Do not silently delete required events.
- Do not claim successful Audit registration.
- Do not admit a Critical request without valid registration
  confirmation.
- Preserve evidence of the failure.
- Report the condition honestly.

No automatic redelivery is authorized.

Unknown or unregistered events must be represented honestly.

Technical details of temporary retention, capacity limits,
and full-capacity behavior remain PROPOSED.

================================================================
24. POLICY CONFLICTS AND EMERGENCY RESPONSE
================================================================

APPROVED

Policy conflicts must be:

- Registered.
- Traceable.
- Referred to the authorized authority.

No irreversible action should proceed while a material
policy conflict remains unresolved.

The decision record must include:

- Reason.
- Evidence.
- Decision-maker.
- Decision time.

Emergency conditions do not waive:

- Security requirements.
- Accounting integrity.
- Audit evidence preservation.
- Zero Automatic Retry.

If safe continuation is impossible, the affected operation
must stop and be escalated to a human authority.

No automatic emergency override is permitted.

25. SAFE HALT AND SAFE RESUME
================================================================

APPROVED

25.1 SAFE HALT PRIORITY

1. Prevent unintended or duplicate financial effects.
2. Preserve Audit evidence and history.
3. Contain security risk.
4. Preserve healthy independent services.
5. Recover only after review, evidence, and authorization.

Safe Halt must:

- Stop only the affected operation or module where possible.
- Preserve its state and evidence.
- Avoid automatic completion or repetition.
- Preserve healthy independent services when safe.

25.2 SAFE RESUME CONDITIONS

Before resumption:

- The cause must be identified.
- Credible evidence must show that the risk is resolved.
- The affected operation state must be determined.
- Repeat-effect risk must be addressed.
- An authorized person must record reason, time, and approval.

Resumption does not authorize repetition of an old operation.

UNKNOWN remains subject to its own governance rules.

25.3 RESUME AUTHORITY

Financial risk:
Finance or accounting lead.

Security risk:
Security lead.

Audit or evidence risk:
Audit lead.

Cross-domain or conflicting authority:
Architecture owner or governance authority designated by
the owner.

No authority may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

25.4 CASE RECORD

Each Stop/Resume case must have an independent traceable case ID.

The case record includes:

- Affected operation and module IDs.
- Reason and stop time.
- Evidence and risk assessment.
- Reviewer and decision-maker.
- Resume or continued-stop decision.
- Decision reason and time.
- Authorization scope.
- Links to prior Audit records.

The case record does not replace original history and does
not automatically change UNKNOWN.

26. FOUR-DATABASE ARCHITECTURE
================================================================

APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

The target topology consists of four physical databases:

1. Evidence Ledger DB.
2. Usage DB.
3. Governance DB.
4. Audit Sink DB.

This is an approved design direction, not implementation
authorization.

Atomicity across the four independent database boundaries
is not guaranteed by the current architecture.

Separate transactions, even when databases share a physical
file, do not by themselves establish a single atomic transaction
across subsystems.

Where Usage and Governance share a physical file, their
observations are not independent.

The current reviewed implementation and target topology must
not be treated as identical.

No database creation, opening, migration, schema modification,
stamping, adoption, backup, restore, or side-effecting test
is authorized by this document.

================================================================
27. D-03: COORDINATED FOUR-DATABASE BACKUP
================================================================

APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

A coordinated backup set must contain:

- Four independent valid database backups.
- Start and end time recorded for each database.
- A central Manifest linking the four backups as one set.

If any required backup is incomplete or invalid, the set
must not be described as complete or valid.

The backup set must not claim that all four backups represent
the same snapshot time.

It must not claim cross-database atomicity.

Restore requires a separate decision and authorization.

This is a design direction only.

No backup, database opening, creation, migration, or restore
operation is authorized.

================================================================
28. D-04: READ-ONLY VERIFIER AND MIGRATION SEPARATION
================================================================

APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

Inspection and verification must be strictly read-only.

Migration must use a separate tool and a separate authorization.

A read-only verifier must not:

- Create databases.
- Change schemas.
- Stamp or adopt ownership.
- Migrate data.
- Repair records.
- Modify database metadata.
- Create side effects through inspection.

Any migration requires its own explicit authorization.

================================================================
29. RESERVATION ACCOUNTING
================================================================

APPROVED PRINCIPLES

Reservations must be separate from actual consumption.

A reservation is not consumption.

Actual usage must be based on reliable evidence.

A failed or UNKNOWN reservation must not automatically be
converted into consumption.

Release of a reservation must be traceable.

Automatic correction or release must not corrupt Usage records.

Technical reservation lifecycle and reconciliation mechanisms
remain subject to separate design and authorization.
