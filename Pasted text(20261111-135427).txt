## 2\. Executive architecture principles (PROPOSED)
1. An initial request is authorized by the request itself, under the existing admission and governance rules. A subsequent attempt for the same logical effect is a separate question.
2. Nothing starts, restarts, or disguises an external effect without a specific, recorded, per-instance authorization for that subsequent attempt. None is currently granted.
3. UNKNOWN is a stable state. It is not failure and not permission to resend.
4. Missing receipts, timeouts, expired leases, and unavailable databases are not proof of failure.
5. Reads (R-2A), same-key resubmission (R-2B), audit redelivery (R-1), repair (R-3), and reservation accounting are separate permissions. Permission in one never implies permission in another.
6. Cross-database eventual consistency is a design direction, not proof of convergence. Disagreements are shown with provenance, never silently repaired.
7. Conservative default: refuse, display, hold. Stuck effects are an accepted cost until the owner decides otherwise.

## 7\. Zero Automatic Retry policy proposal (P1)
**Initial request vs subsequent attempt (clarification, PROPOSED):**
*   **An initially authorized logical effect or request** is the first attempt for an effect, admitted under the existing admission and governance rules. Zero Automatic Retry does not require a separate human authorization for each initial provider request. This document does not claim otherwise.
*   **A subsequent attempt** is any further external write or delivery for the same logical effect (`effect_type + logical_key`). Zero Automatic Retry concerns automatic subsequent attempts.
*   **The initial request's authorization is never authorization for a retry or resubmission.** A subsequent attempt requires its own specific, recorded, per-instance authorization, and the proposed rules below apply to it.
*   The boundary between initial and subsequent attempts depends on the attempt-start boundary (P1-C1). Until P1-C1 is decided, the exact cut-over is UNRESOLVED.

**ZR-DEF (PROPOSED, NOT OWNER-APPROVED):** an automatic retry is any new external write or delivery attempt for the same logical effect (`effect_type + logical_key`) that is a subsequent attempt and is initiated by system logic rather than a specific, recorded, per-instance human authorization.

**Proposed rules:**
*   System-triggered subsequent attempts are prohibited.
*   Timer or scheduler execution of a subsequent attempt is automatic, even when an operator configured it.
*   A human-triggered subsequent attempt is not automatically authorized.
*   Manual resubmission is NOT AUTHORIZED without separate governance.
*   UNKNOWN never becomes failure or resend permission automatically.
*   A late result from the original worker is not a new attempt.
*   Idempotency does not authorize another attempt.
*   Lease expiry, startup replay, and redelivery are automatic if they start a new subsequent external attempt.

**Audit Sink scope (P1-B):**
*   **X1 (existing architectural recommendation, PROPOSED):** Zero Automatic Retry includes Audit Sink. Under X1, S3-2 is a conflict requiring owner direction.
*   **X2 (alternative, UNRESOLVED):** Audit Sink is governed separately, with its own bounded de-duplicated redelivery policy. X2 requires explicit owner approval and a separately defined policy. It is not approved and is not the recommendation.
*   Neither X1 nor X2 is owner-approved.

**Late results vs new attempts:** a late result is recorded with provenance. If a second attempt also occurred, the conflict is shown, not resolved by choosing one.

## 10\. Status query and resubmission policy (P3)
**R-2A, read-only status query (PROPOSED, NOT OWNER-APPROVED):**
*   Access: internal authorized operators only (Q-A).
*   View: composite across four databases with per-field authoritative source and read timestamp. Explicitly not an atomic snapshot (C-1).
*   Shows stale, missing, inconsistent, and unavailable states. Never infers success or failure.
*   Prohibited on the query path: writes, state transitions, recovery, repair, lease reclaim, redelivery, resubmission, provider refresh, automatic correction.
*   Technical enforcement is proposed only. Not claimed to exist.

**R-2B, same-key resubmission (PROPOSED, NOT OWNER-APPROVED):**
*   Option A: strict refusal for an already-started logical effect. Conservative default, not a permanent prohibition.
*   Human operator action does not independently authorize it.
*   The initial request's authorization does not carry over to a resubmission.
*   Future routes stay open through R-3 and explicit governance, without implying they are allowed.

**Read/write separation:** read permission does not grant permission to resend, repair, recover, reclaim, or change state.

**Field ownership:** not invented. Must come from the D-02 record. UNRESOLVED.
## 11\. Repair, recovery, and reservation accounting (P5)
**R-3 repair and recovery (PROPOSED analysis, NOT OWNER-APPROVED):**
*   Internal record repair must not create a new external effect.
*   No automatic UNKNOWN reset.
*   No startup replay without explicit authorization.
*   Disagreements between databases are preserved with provenance. No silent adoption, migration, or reconstruction.
*   Repair may annotate records. It may not infer success or failure.

**Reservation Accounting (UNRESOLVED):**
