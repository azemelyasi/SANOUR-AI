It does not reopen, reverse, or modify previously approved
P1, P2, or P3 decisions.

This revision clarifies:

1. The approval status and provenance of P1, P2, and P3.
2. The distinction between current and historical decision IDs.
3. The distinction between approved decisions and unresolved

2. APPROVED P1 DECISIONS
============================================================

P1-A — ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY DEFINITION

Status: OWNER APPROVED

The initial logical request, when admitted under existing
Admission and Governance rules, constitutes the first attempt.

A subsequent attempt means any additional external write or
delivery for the same logical effect, identified by:

    effect_type + logical_key

The following rules apply:

- The system must not automatically initiate a subsequent
  attempt.
- A Timer- or Scheduler-initiated subsequent attempt is
  automatic, even if its schedule was configured by an
  operator.
- A human action alone does not authorize a subsequent attempt.
- Manual resubmission is not authorized without separate
  Governance authorization.
- UNKNOWN must never automatically become FAILED or create
  permission to resend.
- A late result from the original worker is not a new attempt.
- Idempotency alone does not authorize another attempt.
- Lease expiry, startup replay, and redelivery are subject
  to this rule whenever they initiate a new external effect.
- Each subsequent attempt requires its own specific,
  recorded, per-instance authorization under separate
  Governance rules.

The authorization process, authority structure, storage
mechanism, and technical enforcement of subsequent-attempt
authorization remain unresolved.

Scope of approval:
Architectural definition of Zero Automatic Retry.

Explicitly excluded:
- Implementation of retry or redelivery.
- Implementation of resubmission.
- Modification of existing delivery mechanisms.
- Authorization of any subsequent attempt.
- Changes to Audit Sink operation.


P1-B — AUDIT SINK SCOPE

Status: OWNER APPROVED — OPTION X1

Zero Automatic Retry also applies to Audit Sink delivery.

- Automatic redelivery is prohibited.
- No Audit Sink exception is assumed.
- Any exception, including X2, requires a separate owner
  decision and Governance approval.
- This decision does not authorize modifying, disabling,
  replacing, or operating existing S3-2 mechanisms.


P1-C1 — ATTEMPT START BOUNDARY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

An attempt is considered started when its Attempt Marker
has been durably written.

This is an accounting and Governance boundary only.

The marker alone does not prove:

- That a request was physically transmitted over the network.
- That the Provider received the request.
- That an external effect occurred.

No operational state machine or implementation mechanism
is approved by this definition.


P1-C2 — AUDIT EVENT LOGICAL IDENTITY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

Each internal Audit event has its own independent logical
identity.

This identity is used for internal event identification.

It must not be equated with:

- outbox_key
- payload_digest
- External logical-effect identity

This decision does not authorize bypassing Zero Automatic
Retry or initiating another external effect.

3. APPROVED P2 DECISIONS
============================================================

P2-C1 — LEASE EXPIRY AND RECLAIM

Status: OWNER APPROVED

- Lease expiry alone does not authorize Reclaim that
  initiates a subsequent external effect.
- UNKNOWN must be preserved.
- Any subsequent attempt remains subject to P1-A.
- Lease expiry is not proof of operation failure.

Unresolved:

Whether an internal Claim or Reclaim that does not initiate
an external send may be permitted in a future architecture
has not been decided.

In particular, no assumption may be made that Claim-only
operation is permitted or technically separated from Send
in existing mechanisms.

Until a separate owner decision and authorization exist,
no Claim-only, Reclaim, or operational change is authorized.


P2-C2 — STARTUP REPLAY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

- Automatic replay or resend after restart is prohibited.
- UNKNOWN must be preserved.
- Checking operation status does not authorize resending.
- Startup Replay must not initiate a subsequent external
  attempt.

This decision does not authorize modifying or disabling
existing mechanisms.


P2-D1 — EVIDENCE CRITERIA

Status: OWNER APPROVED

A decision about an operation's status must be based on
credible, traceable evidence linked to that exact operation.

Five approved evidence criteria:

1. Operation identity.
2. Evidence source.
3. Evidence authenticity.
4. Evidence integrity.
5. Evidence time and provenance.

The following are not, by themselves, proof of failure:

- Timeout.
- Lease expiry.
- Missing response.
- Missing record.
- Loss of connectivity to the Provider.

A Provider response indicating ACCEPTED does not necessarily
prove completion of the external effect.

The labels ACCEPTED, COMPLETED, FAILED, and UNKNOWN are
used in this document only to distinguish reported meanings.

No State Machine, Transition rules, or operational mechanism
is approved by these labels.

Unresolved:

Technical and testable definitions of evidence authenticity,
validity, sufficiency, and integrity.


P2-D2 — UNKNOWN DECISION AUTHORITY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

- Final determination of UNKNOWN must be made by an
  authorized human based on evidence.
- A Worker or Scheduler must not determine failure based
  on assumptions, Timeout, or Lease expiry.
- The determination must record the approver, time, reason,
  and evidence.
- A human determination of operation status does not itself
  authorize Resubmission.

Unresolved:

The precise roles, authority levels, and qualification
criteria for an authorized human.


P2-D3 — AUDIT HISTORY PRESERVATION

Status: OWNER APPROVED

- Decision history must not be silently overwritten or
  deleted.
- Corrections and new determinations must be recorded as
  separate, traceable events.

Unresolved:

The storage location, relationship to the four databases,
and technical mechanism for recording corrections.

4. APPROVED P3 DECISIONS — AUDIT EVENT AND DELIVERY
============================================================

P3-A — AUDIT EVENT IDENTITY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

Each Audit Event must have an independent and durable
logical identity, identified in the architectural decision
as:

    audit_event_id

The following identities have distinct purposes:

- audit_event_id: logical identity of the Audit Event.
- outbox_key: delivery identity.
- payload_digest: content-integrity reference.
- External effect identity: identity of the external
  logical effect.

Redelivery must not create a new logical Audit Event.

This decision does not authorize repeating the original
external effect.

Important:

Approval of this definition does not prove that
audit_event_id currently exists or is implemented in code.

Its actual technical existence and implementation status
remain subject to separately authorized verification.

Unresolved:

The exact technical relationship between audit_event_id,
outbox_key, and payload_digest.


P3-B — AUDIT SINK DELIVERY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

- Automatic redelivery is prohibited.
- Audit Event status and delivery status are separate
  concepts.
- An unknown delivery status must remain UNKNOWN.
- Lease expiry does not authorize redelivery.
- Startup Replay does not authorize redelivery.
- Evidence concerning the previous delivery must be
  considered before any separate decision.
- Every subsequent delivery requires independent
  authorization.
- This decision does not authorize modifying, disabling,
  replacing, or operating existing S3-2 mechanisms.

Unresolved:

The names, structure, and storage location of independent
Audit Event and Delivery status fields.


P3-C — MANUAL REDELIVERY AUTHORITY

Status: OWNER APPROVED

Every subsequent Audit Sink delivery requires specific,
recorded human authorization for that exact event and
that exact delivery attempt.

Authorization records must include:

- Approver.
- Time.
- Reason.
- Relevant evidence.

Authorization for one attempt cannot be reused for
subsequent attempts.

Audit Sink redelivery authorization never authorizes
repeating the original external effect.

Unresolved:

- Exact identifier for each delivery attempt.
- Technical single-use authorization consumption mechanism.
- Authorization storage and validation location.
- Prevention of authorization reuse during concurrent
  execution.

No such mechanisms are designed or implemented by this
revision.

8. FOUR-DATABASE ARCHITECTURE
============================================================

The owner-approved target architecture consists of four
physically independent databases:

1. Evidence Ledger DB
2. Usage DB
3. Governance DB
4. Audit Sink DB

This is an approved architectural direction.

It does not establish the current physical implementation
or authorize database creation, migration, or modification.

Previously reported implementation status:

- Usage and Governance shared one physical database in
  the reviewed code.
- Audit Sink was separate by default.
- Evidence Ledger was not wired into the reviewed
  production runtime.

These are historical review findings and must not be
treated as independently reverified current runtime facts.

The approval of four physically independent target
databases does not establish:

- Their current physical existence.
- Their current operational independence.
- Their current production integration.
- Authorization to create or migrate them.

No global atomicity across the four independent database
boundaries is guaranteed by the current architecture.

Separate transactions, even when databases share a
physical file, do not by themselves establish a single
atomic transaction across subsystems.

Where Usage and Governance share a physical file,
their observations are not independent.

No coordinated transaction or cross-database recovery
mechanism is approved by this revision.

10. GOVERNANCE AND IMPLEMENTATION RESTRICTIONS
============================================================

This revision grants NONE of the following permissions:

- Execution of new code.
- Modification of existing code.
- Execution of tests or scripts.
- Migration.
- Creation or opening of any database.
- Schema or metadata modification.
- Backup or Restore.
- Provider connection.
- Real API requests.
- Production activation.
- Modification or removal of S3-2.
- Execution of Redelivery or Resubmission.
- Stage 4 or C4.
- Modification or promotion of Baseline 10.7.
- Changes to public ErrorCodes.
- Modification of the four-database architecture.
- Implementation of any unresolved mechanism.

G1 — DATABASE CREATION

Creation of any database, including temporary databases,
requires explicit authorization for that action.

G2 — SCHEMA MODIFICATION

Any Schema modification, including temporary copies,
requires explicit authorization.

G3 — MIGRATION

Any Migration, including temporary or copied databases,
requires explicit authorization.

G4 — BACKUP AND RESTORE

Backup or Restore involving real data requires explicit
authorization.

G5 — PRODUCTION INTEGRATION

Production integration requires explicit authorization.

G6 — DISPOSABLE OPEN-ORDER TEST

A Disposable Open-Order test requires explicit
authorization.

G7 — BASELINE CHANGE

Any Baseline modification or promotion requires explicit
authorization.

G8 — IMPLEMENTATION PHASE AUTHORIZATION

Every implementation phase requires separate, explicit
owner approval.

This applies even to read-only or test-only implementation
phases.

Approval of one phase does not authorize the next phase.

No authorization may be inferred from a previous approval
unless its scope explicitly includes the action in question.

AUTHORIZATION SCOPE RULE

Every authorization must identify its specific scope,
phase, and permitted action.

Authorization for one action does not automatically extend
to another action or a later phase.

Database restrictions G1 through G7 remain independently
applicable in addition to G8.
