0. DOCUMENT PURPOSE, AUTHORITY AND STATUS
================================================================

0.1 PURPOSE

This document defines the integrated safety, control,
human governance, ownership protection, emergency response,
audit, evidence, and operational governance principles
for SANOUR.

Its purpose is to ensure that:

- AI systems remain within authorized boundaries.
- Independent safety controls cannot be bypassed by AI.
- Final governance authority remains outside AI control.
- Sensitive operations require valid authorization.
- External effects are not repeated automatically.
- UNKNOWN outcomes are not converted into assumed failures.
- Audit evidence and decision history are preserved.
- Ownership and succession remain under independent
  human governance.
- Technical implementation remains subject to separate
  authorization.

0.2 RELATIONSHIP TO THE MASTER DECISION RECORD

This document is a companion to:

SANOUR_MASTER_ARCHITECTURE_DECISIONS_r4.5.3

The master record documents architectural decisions,
their provenance, historical identifiers, and implementation
restrictions.

This document consolidates the safety and governance
architecture into one integrated reference.

It does not replace the master decision history.

It must not silently modify, reverse, or reinterpret
previously approved owner decisions.

0.3 STATUS DEFINITIONS

APPROVED:
Explicitly approved by the project owner within the
stated scope.

PROPOSED:
A recommended direction that has not received separate
explicit owner approval.

UNRESOLVED:

An action for which the required implementation or

1. Approval of an architectural principle.

1. FOUNDATIONAL AI SAFETY AND CONTROL PRINCIPLE
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

No AI system, Agent, autonomous process, or AI-controlled
component may have authority to change, remove, weaken,
or bypass SANOUR's independent control and safety
mechanisms.

Final governance authority remains outside AI control.

No AI system or Agent may independently:

- Change foundational security policies.
- Increase its own privileges.
- Grant itself additional permissions.
- Create an Agent with greater privileges without
  authorization.
- Disable independent safety controls.
- Bypass permission checks.
- Appoint or replace the system owner.
- Change ownership succession rules.
- Authorize its own sensitive operations.
- Remove or weaken Zero Automatic Retry.
- Authorize its own restart after a dangerous incident.
- Select the final authority in a human governance dispute.

AI recommendations are not authorization.

AI-generated plans are not authorization.

AI-generated approval statements are not authorization.

An AI component cannot use its own output as proof that
a sensitive action has been approved.

3. INDEPENDENT EMERGENCY SAFETY LAYER
================================================================

3.1 APPROVED PRINCIPLES

SANOUR must have independent safety controls capable of
limiting or stopping AI access to sensitive tools, network
paths, and operations.

AI systems and Agents must not be able to disable, weaken,
or bypass these controls.

The safety architecture must support:

- Independent restriction of sensitive operations.
- Human-authorized recovery.
- Evidence preservation.
- Protection against privilege escalation.
- Proportional containment.
- Continued operation of healthy independent services
  where safe.

3.2 PROPOSED TECHNICAL CHARACTERISTICS

The Emergency Safety Layer should be:

- Independent of AI decision-making.
- Bounded in authority.
- Small and understandable where practical.
- Protected against unauthorized modification.
- Designed to avoid a single point of failure.

The exact implementation, hardware, process isolation,
redundancy, and deployment model remain unresolved.

3.3 AUTHORITY LIMITS

The Emergency Safety Layer may enforce approved safety
restrictions.

It must not independently appoint a new owner, change
foundational governance policy, or create new human
authority.

Its technical authority must remain within the boundaries
approved by human governance.

================================================================
4. PROPORTIONAL SAFETY AND NORMAL USER ACCESS
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Safety controls must focus on AI and high-risk operations
without unnecessarily blocking ordinary users or healthy
services.

Restrictions should affect the smallest safe scope.

Preferred containment order:

1. Affected Agent.
2. Affected Provider or execution path.
3. Affected sensitive operation or module.
4. Wider system restriction only when the risk requires it.

A restriction record should identify:

- Cause.
- Affected scope.
- Start time.
- Remaining permitted functions.
- Responsible authority.
- Review conditions.
- Conditions for lifting the restriction.

A restriction must not be reported as a full-system stop
when healthy independent services remain operational.

================================================================
5. FAIL SAFE, NOT FAIL OPEN
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

If independent safety controls fail:

- Sensitive operations must stop or remain restricted.
- Agents must not acquire new privileges.
- Providers and tools must not perform new sensitive
  operations without valid authorization.
- Evidence must be preserved.
- Healthy independent low-risk services may continue
  where safe.
- Sensitive operations may resume only after review and
  appropriate human authorization.

Safety-control failure must never automatically grant
additional permissions.

Avoiding a single point of failure is an approved
architectural objective.

The exact redundancy and failover implementation remains
subject to technical design and separate authorization.

================================================================
6. ADMINISTRATIVE CONTROL
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED PRINCIPLES

An ordinary administrator cannot disable the Emergency
Safety Layer.

An administrator cannot independently:

- Remove foundational safety controls.
- Weaken Zero Automatic Retry.
- Transfer permanent ownership.
- Appoint a new owner.
- Authorize AI to bypass safety boundaries.
- Remove all independent safety mechanisms.

Foundational safety-policy changes require owner or
owner-designated governance approval.

Highly sensitive operations should require two independent
human approvals where feasible.

Important administrative decisions must be recorded.

Detailed roles, authentication methods, permission
matrices, and technical enforcement remain subject to
separate design.

================================================================
7. OWNER ACCOUNT PROTECTION
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Owner-account protection must include:

- Strong multi-factor authentication.
- Re-authentication for critical changes.
- Independent offline recovery keys.
- Security delays for highly sensitive changes, with
  an opportunity to cancel.
- Recording of requests, approvals, and outcomes.
- Suspension of suspicious critical changes pending
  human review.

Protection must not create unnecessary friction for
ordinary user access.

AI and Agents cannot modify owner-account protection rules.

Detailed authentication and recovery technology remain
subject to technical design.

================================================================
8. EMERGENCY OWNER RECOVERY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Emergency owner recovery must be independent of AI
and Agents.

It must include:

- Secure offline recovery keys.
- Multiple independent verification factors.
- Verifiable evidence of the recovery request.
- Protection against ordinary administrator or Agent
  takeover.
- Traceable recovery records.

No critical ownership transfer may proceed until
verification requirements are satisfied.

An AI Agent cannot appoint a new owner through recovery.

Emergency recovery and permanent ownership succession
are separate processes.

================================================================
9. EMERGENCY SUCCESSION
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

The owner may designate an emergency successor or
trustee in advance.

Emergency succession authority is limited.

It does not automatically grant:

- Permanent ownership.
- Permanent owner appointment authority.
- Authority to weaken foundational safety policies.
- Authority to bypass Zero Automatic Retry.
- Unlimited privilege expansion.

Activation requires:

1. Verification of owner unavailability under predefined
   conditions.
2. Independent verification of the successor's identity.
3. Independent human approval.

Termination also requires independent human approval.

The owner's return does not automatically erase or transfer
previously recorded emergency decisions.

AI cannot activate, cancel, or modify emergency succession.

The exact legal conditions and jurisdiction remain
UNRESOLVED.

================================================================
10. PERMANENT OWNERSHIP TRANSFER
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Emergency succession does not automatically become
permanent ownership.

Permanent ownership transfer requires:

- A valid, pre-approved succession document.
- Independent evidence.
- Verification of applicable legal requirements.
- Review by the independent succession authority.

If the required document is missing, invalid, or disputed,
the transfer must stop.

AI systems, Agents, and ordinary administrators cannot
appoint a permanent owner.

Applicable law must be followed.

================================================================
11. INDEPENDENT PERMANENT SUCCESSION AUTHORITY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED PRINCIPLES

The permanent succession authority must be independent of:

- AI systems and Agents.
- Ordinary administrators.
- The person applying for ownership transfer.

Its responsibilities include:

- Reviewing succession documents.
- Reviewing legal and identity evidence.
- Stopping a transfer when evidence is insufficient
  or disputed.
- Recording its decision and supporting evidence.

Succession authority does not automatically grant
permission to change foundational safety policies.

Legal constitution, jurisdiction, and formal establishment
remain UNRESOLVED.

================================================================
12. SUCCESSION AUTHORITY MEMBERSHIP
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Membership structure:

- Three principal members.
- Two designated alternate members.

The owner appoints the initial members.

AI systems have no role in selecting, dismissing, or
replacing members.

Permanent ownership transfer requires at least two
affirmative votes from three valid and eligible principal
members.

No single member can independently approve a transfer.

A member with a relevant conflict of interest cannot vote
on the affected matter.

If fewer than the required valid members are available,
the transfer must stop.

Independent identity verification and authentication
are required.

Multiple accounts alone do not establish independent
identity.

Shared credentials are prohibited.

Agents cannot vote.

Credible collusion concerns require suspension and
independent human review.

================================================================
13. MEMBER QUALIFICATION
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Members must:

- Have verifiable real identities.
- Declare relevant conflicts of interest.
- Understand their legal and security duties.
- Use independent accounts and authentication.
- Maintain their qualifications.
- Avoid delegating their vote to an Agent or another person.

Family, friendship, or professional relationships alone
neither qualify nor disqualify a person.

Actual independence and conflicts must be assessed.

================================================================
14. RESIGNATION, REMOVAL, AND REPLACEMENT
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Resignation must be formally recorded.

Death, incapacity, or loss of qualification triggers review.

Removal requires documented grounds and an independent
process.

No member may unilaterally remove another member.

A member under review is excluded from the affected
sensitive decision.

Membership changes must be recorded while preserving
previous records.

If the number of valid members is insufficient,
permanent ownership transfer stops.

An alternate first completes the remaining term.

A new independent five-year term may begin only after
valid selection.

================================================================
15. MEMBER TERMS
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Principal member term: Five years.

One renewal is permitted after independent evaluation.

Qualification and conflict-of-interest reviews occur
annually.

Alternates must maintain their qualifications.

Expiry does not automatically create new authority.

If valid membership is incomplete, a sensitive transfer
cannot proceed.

================================================================
16. GOVERNANCE DISAGREEMENT
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

No member may independently execute a disputed permanent
ownership transfer.

The request is suspended pending resolution.

The process must:

- Preserve evidence.
- Preserve objections and reasons.
- Escalate to the appropriate higher governance authority.
- Record decisions and responsible persons.

Silence or passage of time is not approval.

Unresolved disagreement means the sensitive operation
does not proceed.

Healthy independent services may continue where safe.

AI cannot select the winning position or resolve the
dispute by itself.

17. ZERO AUTOMATIC RETRY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED — P1-A / ZR-DEF

17.1 INITIAL ATTEMPT

The initial authorized logical request, when admitted
under existing Admission and Governance rules, constitutes
the first attempt.

17.2 SUBSEQUENT ATTEMPT

A subsequent attempt means any additional external write
or delivery for the same logical effect, identified by:

    effect_type + logical_key

17.3 PROHIBITION

The system must not automatically initiate a subsequent
attempt.

This includes attempts initiated by:

- Timers.
- Schedulers.
- Startup replay.
- Lease expiry.
- Automatic redelivery.
- Automatic recovery.
- Background workers.
- Automatic resubmission.

A Timer- or Scheduler-initiated attempt remains automatic
even if an operator configured its schedule.

17.4 HUMAN AUTHORIZATION

A human action alone does not authorize a subsequent
attempt.

Manual resubmission is not authorized without separate
Governance authorization.

Each subsequent attempt requires its own specific,
recorded, per-instance authorization.

The authorization process, authority structure, storage
mechanism, and technical enforcement remain unresolved.

17.5 UNKNOWN

UNKNOWN must never automatically become FAILED or create
permission to resend.

Timeout, lease expiry, missing response, or missing
receipt does not authorize another attempt.

17.6 LATE RESULT

A late result from the original worker is not a new attempt.

17.7 IDEMPOTENCY

Idempotency alone does not authorize another attempt.

An unchanged idempotency key does not grant permission
for a subsequent external effect.

17.8 SCOPE

Zero Automatic Retry applies to original external effects
and Audit Sink delivery.

No exception is implied.

17.9 EXPLICIT EXCLUSIONS

Approval of P1-A does not authorize:

- Retry implementation.
- Redelivery implementation.
- Resubmission implementation.
- Changes to existing delivery mechanisms.
- Any subsequent attempt.
- Changes to Audit Sink operation.

18. ATTEMPT START BOUNDARY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED — P1-C1

An attempt is considered started when its Attempt Marker
has been durably written.

This is an accounting and Governance boundary only.

The marker alone does not prove:

- Physical network transmission.
- Provider receipt.
- Completion of an external effect.

No operational state machine or implementation mechanism
is approved by this definition.

================================================================
19. P1-A AND P3-C AUTHORIZATION SCOPE DISTINCTION
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED CLARIFICATION

P1-A concerns authorization for a subsequent external
attempt for the same logical effect.

P3-C concerns authorization for a subsequent Audit Sink
delivery for a specific Audit Event and delivery attempt.

These authorizations have different scopes and purposes.

An authorization under P1-A does not automatically
authorize Audit Sink redelivery under P3-C.

An authorization under P3-C does not authorize repetition
of the original external logical effect under P1-A.

Whether a single authorization record may explicitly
document both authorization scopes remains unresolved.

Until a separate owner decision establishes otherwise,
the two authorization requirements must be treated
as separate.

No authorization may be inferred from the existence
of the other authorization.

No authorization mechanism, storage structure, or
implementation is approved by this clarification.

26. EVIDENCE CRITERIA
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED — P2-D1

A decision about an operation's status must be based on
credible, traceable evidence linked to that exact operation.

Five approved evidence criteria:

1. Operation identity.
2. Evidence source.
3. Evidence authenticity.
4. Evidence integrity.
5. Evidence time and provenance.

The following are not, by themselves, proof of failure:

- Timeout.
- Lease expiry.
- Missing response.
- Missing record.
- Loss of connectivity to the Provider.
- Database unavailability.

A Provider response indicating ACCEPTED does not
necessarily prove completion of the external effect.

The labels:

ACCEPTED
COMPLETED
FAILED
UNKNOWN

distinguish reported meanings only.

They do not approve a state machine, transition rules,
or operational mechanism.

Technical and testable definitions of evidence authenticity,
validity, sufficiency, and integrity remain unresolved.

29. R-2A: READ-ONLY STATUS AND HISTORY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

29.1 PURPOSE AND ACCESS

R-2A provides a read-only composite view of operation
status and history across the four target databases.

Access is limited to authorized internal operators
according to approved access permissions.

29.2 STRICT READ-ONLY BOUNDARY

R-2A must not perform:

- Writes.
- State transitions.
- Recovery.
- Repair.
- Lease reclaim.
- Redelivery.
- Resubmission.
- Provider refresh.
- Automatic correction.

Viewing information does not authorize changing it.

29.3 PER-FIELD SOURCE AUTHORITY

For every displayed field, identify:

- Authoritative source.
- Source database.
- Last observation timestamp.
- Completeness.
- Availability.
- Freshness.

A stale observation must never be presented as live.

An inaccessible database must be shown as unavailable.

The system must not infer missing information from the
remaining three databases.

29.4 INCONSISTENT INFORMATION

If sources disagree:

- Show each source's information separately.
- Mark the composite result INCONSISTENT.
- Do not automatically select a winning source.
- Do not select the newest timestamp as the winner
  merely because it is newer.
- Require separate review and evidence for adjudication.

29.5 OPERATION INFORMATION

Where available, show:

- Unique operation ID.
- Reported status.
- Source database for each field.
- Last observation time.
- Completeness.
- Disagreement among sources.
- Availability of each database.
- UNKNOWN status without automatic interpretation.

29.6 TRACEABLE HISTORY

Display separately:

- Initial request registration time.
- Attempt-start time.
- Provider response, if present.
- Audit Event registration time.
- Last observation time.
- Human UNKNOWN decision.
- Decision reason and evidence.

Every event must identify its source and time.

No inferred event may be displayed as confirmed.

29.7 ROLE-BASED ACCESS

Approved role direction:

Viewer:
Permitted viewing information only.

Auditor:
Permitted information and Audit history.

Administrator:
Permitted overall status and management reports.

These roles do not acquire permission to change,
retry, repair, redeliver, or recover operations merely
by viewing them.

Detailed authentication, role definitions, and permission
matrices remain subject to separate design.

29.8 ACCESS AUDIT

Access to sensitive information must itself be auditable.

Record:

- User ID.
- Access time.
- Data type.
- Operation ID, where applicable.
- Allow or deny result.

Avoid placing unnecessary sensitive data in access logs.

Access logging does not change operation status.

29.9 NON-ATOMIC COMPOSITE VIEW

A composite view across four independent databases
must not be represented as an atomic snapshot unless
a separately approved and verified mechanism establishes
that property.

Each source observation must retain its own provenance
and timestamp.

30. FOUR-DATABASE TARGET ARCHITECTURE
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

The target topology consists of four physically
independent databases:

1. Evidence Ledger DB.
2. Usage DB.
3. Governance DB.
4. Audit Sink DB.

This is an approved design direction, not implementation
authorization.

Atomicity across the four independent database boundaries
is not guaranteed by the current architecture.

Separate transactions, even when databases share a
physical file, do not by themselves establish a single
atomic transaction across subsystems.

Where Usage and Governance share a physical file,
their observations are not independent.

Previously reviewed implementation status:

- Usage and Governance shared one physical database
  in the reviewed code.
- Audit Sink was separate by default.
- Evidence Ledger was not wired into the reviewed
  production runtime.

These are historical review findings and must not be
treated as independently reverified current runtime facts.

Approval of the target topology does not establish:

- Current physical existence.
- Current operational independence.
- Current production integration.
- Permission to create or migrate databases.

No global atomicity or cross-database recovery mechanism
is approved by this document.

31. D-03: COORDINATED FOUR-DATABASE BACKUP
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED DESIGN DIRECTION

A coordinated backup set must contain:

- Four independent valid database backups.
- Start time recorded for each database.
- End time recorded for each database.
- A central Manifest linking the four backups as one set.

The backup set must not claim that all four backups
represent the same snapshot time.

It must not claim cross-database atomicity.

Restore requires a separate decision and authorization.

This is a design direction only.

No backup, database opening, creation, migration,
or restore operation is authorized.

================================================================
34. AUDIT CONFIDENTIALITY AND INTEGRITY
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED PRINCIPLES

34.1 CONFIDENTIALITY

Audit information is available only to authorized persons.

API keys, passwords, and authentication secrets must
never be stored raw in Audit records.

Unnecessary sensitive information must be avoided.

Secure identifiers and redaction should be used where
needed.

Access to sensitive Audit information is itself auditable.

34.2 INTEGRITY

SANOUR must be able to detect potential unauthorized
modification or deletion of Audit records.

Cryptographic chaining or an equivalent integrity
mechanism is an approved architectural direction.

The exact algorithm, key management, storage arrangement,
and verification implementation remain unresolved.

Integrity checks must produce traceable results.

Detection does not automatically authorize repair,
deletion, or correction.

34.3 INTEGRITY INCIDENT

When potential tampering or integrity failure is detected:

- Record INTEGRITY_ALERT.
- Notify the security lead or authorized auditor.
- Preserve suspect records.
- Do not silently delete or overwrite records.
- Do not infer the actor or cause without evidence.
- Do not perform automatic repair or correction.

Remediation requires evidence and appropriate

35. AUDIT EVENT CLASSIFICATION
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

Every event type must have a defined classification:

CRITICAL
IMPORTANT
INFORMATIONAL

No event is automatically Critical merely because it is
important to a particular module.

35.1 CRITICAL EVENTS

The approved Critical list is:

1. Admission of financial/accounting requests.
2. Registration of an external effect, such as payment
   or transfer.
3. Human decision to resolve UNKNOWN.
4. Issuance of authorization for a subsequent attempt
   or redelivery.
5. Changes to roles or security permissions.
6. Detection of tampering or integrity failure
   (INTEGRITY_ALERT).

If valid Audit Sink registration confirmation is absent
for a Critical event, the related request must not
be admitted.

35.2 IMPORTANT EVENTS

The approved Important list is:

1. Non-critical system configuration changes.
2. Customer or supplier contact information changes.
3. Operational errors without financial-effect stoppage.
4. Reporting configuration changes.
5. User login and logout.
6. Non-sensitive management information changes.
7. Capacity and performance alerts.
8. Module version or configuration changes.

35.3 INFORMATIONAL EVENTS

The approved Informational list is:

1. Viewing general reports.
2. Viewing statistical dashboards.
3. Producing periodic reports.
4. Resource usage and performance statistics.
5. General system notifications.
6. Non-sensitive diagnostic events.

35.4 CLASSIFICATION GOVERNANCE

Every event type must have a defined level.

Changing a classification requires a reason and approval.

If an event is tied to admission of a financial or
security request, Critical requirements apply.

Historical events must not be overwritten.

Classification history must remain traceable.

Overall policy changes require approval by the SANOUR
architecture owner.

A security lead may propose a change but cannot approve
it alone.

The record must include:

- Reason.
- Effective date.
- Policy version.
- Approving authority.

Lowering a Critical classification requires special
review and explicit owner approval.

Review occurs every six months and additionally after
a major security incident, architecture change, or
newly identified risk.

A review does not itself change event classifications.

The review outcome must be recorded even when no change
is made.

38. SAFE HALT AND SAFE RESUME
================================================================

STATUS: OWNER APPROVED

38.1 SAFE HALT PRIORITY

1. Prevent unintended or duplicate financial effects.
2. Preserve Audit evidence and history.
3. Contain security risk.
4. Preserve healthy independent services.
5. Recover only after review, evidence, and authorization.

Safe Halt must:

- Stop only the affected operation or module where
  possible.
- Preserve its state and evidence.
- Avoid automatic completion or repetition.
- Preserve healthy independent services when safe.

38.2 SAFE RESUME CONDITIONS

Before resumption:

- The cause must be identified.
- Credible evidence must show that the risk is resolved.
- The affected operation state must be determined.
- Repeat-effect risk must be addressed.
- An authorized person must record reason, time,
  and approval.

Resumption does not authorize repetition of an old
operation.

UNKNOWN remains subject to its own governance rules.

38.3 RESUME AUTHORITY

Financial risk:
Finance or accounting lead.

Security risk:
Security lead.

Audit or evidence risk:
Audit lead.

Cross-domain or conflicting authority:
Architecture owner or governance authority designated
by the owner.

No authority may bypass Zero Automatic Retry.

38.4 CASE RECORD

Each Stop/Resume case must have an independent
traceable case ID.

The case record includes:

- Affected operation and module IDs.
- Reason and stop time.
- Evidence and risk assessment.
- Reviewer and decision-maker.
- Resume or continued-stop decision.
- Decision reason and time.
- Authorization scope.
- Links to prior Audit records.

The case record does not replace original history
and does not automatically change UNKNOWN.
